# 2026-05-13 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
The todo completion agent automatically researches, drafts, and schedules when you type a simple checkbox, eliminating the gap between task creation and execution. Same pattern works for any business with recurring research or coordination tasks.

## Patterns

### 1. The todo completion agent that works while you plan
*(tags: `consultants`, `small-law-firms`, `accountants`, `real-estate`)*

Char, from YC S26, turns a simple notepad into an autonomous research assistant where typing a checkbox triggers an agent that researches, drafts, or schedules on your behalf. The agent monitors your daily notepad for checkbox entries, analyzes task requirements against available data sources, executes research through web search and document retrieval, drafts responses or creates calendar entries, and presents completed work for review. This replaces the mental overhead of task management and the time spent switching between planning and execution ([YC S26, May 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/char)).

At a small law firm managing client matters, the same mechanism triggers when attorneys add case research items to their daily planning notes. The agent reads task descriptions and case context, searches legal databases and precedent collections, identifies relevant statutes and case law, drafts research summaries with proper citations, and creates client communication templates. It knows which databases contain the most current information, which precedents are binding in specific jurisdictions, and when to escalate complex legal questions to senior partners. Stack is case management system integration plus legal research database APIs plus Claude for analysis plus automated document generation. Around $600-900 per month for multi-partner practices. Replaces the associate attorney spending 10 hours weekly on routine legal research and the paralegal coordinating research requests across active matters. Pitch: your case research happens automatically from planning notes to completed memos, precedent searches stay current across all jurisdictions, and billable hours focus on legal strategy instead of information gathering.

At a real estate investment firm managing property acquisitions, the agent handles market research, comparable sales analysis, and due diligence coordination. It processes property research requests from acquisition notes, gathers market data and comparable sales, coordinates inspections and appraisals, and prepares investment analysis summaries. Around $500-800 per month for multi-property investors. Replaces the analyst managing research workflows and the coordinator tracking due diligence tasks.

### 2. The event sourcing agent that builds attendance while you focus on content
*(tags: `consultants`, `restaurants`, `gyms`, `retail`)*

Pavoot, from YC S26, helps marketing and sales teams source the right attendees, personalize each guest's experience, and turn events into pipeline through automated attendee research and engagement. The agent triggers on event creation in marketing systems, reads event parameters and target audience criteria, sources qualified attendees from professional networks and company databases, personalizes invitations based on attendee profiles and interests, and manages follow-up sequences to convert attendees into qualified leads. This replaces the event coordinator manually researching prospects and the marketing manager creating generic outreach that misses conversion opportunities ([YC S26, May 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/pavoot)).

At a restaurant hosting wine tastings or chef events, the same mechanism triggers when special events are added to the booking system. The agent reads event details and capacity requirements, identifies customers from reservation history who ordered similar wines or premium dishes, creates personalized invitations highlighting relevant menu items and past dining preferences, manages RSVP tracking and waitlist coordination, and follows up with attendees for feedback and future booking opportunities. It knows which customers prefer which wine varietals, which events historically drove the highest per-person spend, and when to upsell premium experiences or wine purchases. Stack is reservation system integration plus customer database access plus Claude for personalization plus email automation. Around $400-600 per month for restaurants running regular events. Replaces the manager spending 8 hours weekly coordinating events and the server manually tracking customer preferences for special occasions. Pitch: your events fill with customers who actually want to be there, every invitation feels personally crafted, and attendees convert to higher-value repeat bookings.

At a gym or fitness studio running specialty classes and workshops, the agent sources participants based on workout history, creates targeted promotions for specific fitness interests, and manages class capacity optimization. Around $300-500 per month for multi-program fitness businesses. Replaces the marketing coordinator managing event promotion and the front desk staff tracking member preferences for special programming.

### 3. The personal broadcast agent that turns your day into content
*(tags: `consultants`, `accountants`, `small-law-firms`, `medical-practices`)*

Spotify launched AI agent integration in May 2026 allowing agents like Claude Code and OpenClaw to generate and upload personal podcasts from daily digests, class notes, and calendar events. The agent triggers on scheduled intervals or calendar updates, reads calendar appointments and meeting notes, synthesizes content into structured audio narratives, generates personalized briefings with key insights and action items, and uploads completed podcasts to personal libraries for review during commute or downtime. This replaces the manual effort to review and synthesize daily work and the mental overhead of staying current on project status across multiple clients or cases ([Spotify AI Agents, May 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/07/spotify-personal-podcasts-ai-agents/)).

At a consulting firm managing multiple client engagements, the same mechanism triggers at the end of each business day to process meeting notes and project updates. The agent reads client meeting transcripts and project status reports, synthesizes key decisions and action items across all active engagements, creates audio summaries organized by client priority and deadline urgency, highlights potential conflicts or resource constraints, and delivers daily briefings that partners can consume while traveling between client sites. It knows which clients require weekly executive updates, which projects are approaching critical milestones, and when to escalate timeline risks to senior management. Stack is meeting transcription software plus project management system integration plus Claude for synthesis plus audio generation APIs. Around $500-800 per month for multi-partner firms. Replaces the project manager spending 5 hours weekly creating status reports and the partner spending time manually reviewing project status across concurrent engagements. Pitch: you stay current on every client engagement without reading reports, critical decisions surface automatically during your commute, and project risk identification happens before problems cascade across multiple accounts.

At a medical practice managing patient cases and continuing education requirements, the agent creates personalized medical briefings from patient notes, research updates, and CME content. Around $400-600 per month for multi-provider practices. Replaces the physician manually reviewing case notes and the office manager tracking continuing education progress.
