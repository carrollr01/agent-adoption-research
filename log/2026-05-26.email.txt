Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-05-26 — 3 patterns | SMBs selling to AI shopping agents

https://github.com/ryanhcarroll/agent-adoption-research/blob/main/log/2026-05-26.md

Three patterns today. The most consequential is Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite, launched May 19, alongside parallel rollouts from BigCommerce and Shopify's Agentic Plan. The mechanism is mechanically simple but structurally new: a merchant connects their product catalog to Stripe or Shopify or BigCommerce, picks which AI agents they want to sell through (ChatGPT, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, PayPal), and the platform pushes structured product data into those agent surfaces. When a consumer's AI agent decides to buy, it uses a Shared Payment Token to pass credentials, the merchant takes the order through their existing stack, and stays merchant-of-record for refunds and disputes. URBN, Etsy, Coach are early users, but Shopify is explicitly pitching this as how a five-person skincare brand gets discovered in ChatGPT alongside Sephora. The direct copy is into Sollos and any pre-launch CPG: a one-week catalog audit and rewrite that scores each product on agent-readability and rebuilds the structured fields so the brand shows up when ChatGPT helps a shopper find an alternative to Liquid Death. Same mechanism for restaurants selling merch, music studios selling accessories, any SMB with a Shopify or BigCommerce catalog.

The second pattern is Canary Technologies launching the Hospitality AI Agent Studio for its 20,000+ hotel customers (Marriott, Four Seasons, Choice, Wyndham, BWH, IHG). Canary didn't ship a feature; it shipped a studio — pre-built configurable agent templates for Front Desk, Concierge, and Central Reservations, plus the ability for hoteliers to define their own. The hotelier writes the SOP once in plain language, the agent enforces it across every channel. This is the meta-pattern: vertical SaaS will eventually ship configurable agent studios in every sector, and the gap between now and then is where A3 Dev plays. For Jenn and the IEC market, no equivalent has shipped — CollegePlannerPro, CounselMore, and GradBetter all ship features, not studios. The trigger is a new family inquiry; the agent reads the IEC's policy file ("don't take families targeting Ivies only; sweet spot is 1300-1450 SAT, top 50 schools"), and either books a discovery call or sends a polite decline with referrals. Same template into music studios (Justin's Squarespace form into Opus1) and into SAT/ACT prep firms (Debbie's intake to diagnostic). The IP A3 Dev builds is the SOP-file-as-config-layer, not the agent itself.

The third pattern is the ambient coach agent, hardening into a real category. Avoca, $1B valuation, deployed across pest control, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage doors. Their Coach product listens to every call a human CSR takes, scores it against the company's playbook (objection handling, empathy markers, filler-word count, booking attempts), and surfaces flagged moments to the CSR and the manager. Rilla Live does the same for field reps — manager opens a dashboard, sees the live transcript of an in-home pitch, pushes a coaching note to the rep's phone mid-conversation. Home Depot is using it. Granite Comfort rebuilt nine brands around Avoca's Responder, Coach, and human-in-the-loop pairing. The mechanism is the inverse of the voice-agent pattern. The agent doesn't take the call. The human takes the call; the agent listens, compares against a playbook, prompts the human in real time or queues a coaching card for the manager. The interesting copy isn't into another trade — it's into the verticals where the human conversation is the product. For Jenn, the trigger is her discovery call; the agent listens with consent, scores against her own playbook ("did I anchor expectations before quoting fees; did I ask about the kid vs the parent's projection"), and surfaces a weekly digest of where she's drifting from her best calls. For Debbie's tutors at Capital Educators, the agent scores diagnostic-to-enrollment conversion calls across the whole team. For Justin selling lesson packages on tours, same pattern, same scoring. Stack is Otter or Fathom for transcription plus Claude API for scoring plus a daily email digest. The hard part isn't the tech — it's writing the playbook. That's the deliverable A3 Dev sells.
