Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-05-04 — 3 patterns | Long-running research agent replaces $12k analyst week with $200 compute

https://github.com/RCarroll6/agent-adoption-research

OffDeal, an AI-native investment bank for $1M-$20M EBITDA businesses, consolidated its work into a single Claude-based agent named Archie hooked into more than twenty data sources. The mechanism that matters: Archie runs autonomously for up to four hours, sweeps every permutation of sector, geography, and acquisition history, and returns a finished buyer list. A list that used to cost a team of analysts a week and roughly $12,000 to produce comes back for about $200 in compute. After moving to the Claude Agent SDK their eval accuracy went from 25% to 85%, each banker now runs five to eight concurrent deals, and the firm closed eight deals worth $91M with two people.

The pattern is not reactive chatbot. It is a scheduled or on-demand research job that takes hours, sweeps a search space exhaustively, and drops a finished artifact in the morning. A small business broker selling HVAC shops or accounting practices can run it Sunday night to find roll-up buyers for a $2M EBITDA book. Stack is the Claude Agent SDK or n8n with web research tools plus the broker's CRM. Around $300/month including compute. Replaces the analyst the broker can't afford to hire. A boutique technical recruiter runs the same mechanism Monday morning to find sixty candidates for a niche role, ranked by precision-of-fit. Around $200/month. Replaces two days of Boolean LinkedIn hunting.

Pattern two came out of How I AI's Cortney Hickey episode. Cortney is the EA to Zapier's CEO, and she built a custom GPT trained on a document called "Managing Up to Wade" — explicit notes on the CEO's communication style, his strategic priorities, his pet peeves. Anyone at Zapier can upload a draft memo and get it pre-stress-tested before scheduling a meeting. Encode the boss's preferences once; everyone runs their drafts through it forever. At a two-partner CPA firm, the same mechanism becomes a "Managing Up to Sarah" GPT trained on partner-style prose and ten exemplar redlined memos. ChatGPT Team or Claude Projects plus Google Drive for the knowledge base. Around $30/seat/month. Replaces the partner's 9 PM redline cycle. The pitch is "every memo lands on the partner's desk pre-redlined to their standards, so review takes thirty minutes instead of two hours of rewriting."

Pattern three is GC AI's Playbook mechanism, now live at 1,500 in-house legal teams across 53 countries with a fresh ROI study showing 14 hours/week saved per lawyer. The product encodes the team's preferred contract positions as a runnable playbook, then runs every inbound contract through it clause by clause, redlines against the team's standards, and cites exact source language. The mechanism is "encode the checklist as a runnable playbook; point it at every inbound document; get back a clause-level redline with citations." A two-broker independent insurance agency runs the same pattern on every inbound certificate of insurance — checks required limits, additional-insured language, primary/non-contributory wording — and emails the carrier with the asks. Stack is the AMS API plus Claude with a structured playbook prompt plus Microsoft 365. Around $300-500/month. Replaces the producer's Friday afternoon "let me look at this COI" pile.

Connecting thread: all three patterns are about encoding what someone already knows — the partner's redline preferences, the firm's contract positions, the broker's buyer-search heuristics — and turning that knowledge into a runnable artifact that anyone in the org can invoke. Less "build me a chatbot" and more "package my judgment so the rest of the team can use it without me being in the room."
