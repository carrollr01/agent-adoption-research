# 2026-05-08 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
Marco Casalaina (Microsoft VP of Core AI) is publicly running "ad-hoc micro-agents" — small triggered workflows he spins up in 30 seconds with a natural-language prompt instead of building one big assistant. Strongest fit for Jenn: a fleet of single-purpose agents (parent meeting requests, deadline nudges, school-list updates) instead of one omnibus IEC tool.

## Patterns

### 1. The 30-second micro-agent that replaces one specific email reply — `jenn`
**Source:** [How I AI episode with Marco Casalaina (Microsoft VP, Core AI Products)](https://www.chatprd.ai/how-i-ai/microsofts-ai-vp-automates-everything-with-5-micro-agent-workflows) — Casalaina demonstrates building a triggered M365 Copilot "Workflows" agent live by typing one sentence: *"When I get an email from Claire Vo requesting a meeting at a certain time, check my calendar. If that time is free, send her a 30-minute meeting invite for that time."* He calls these "ad-hoc agents" or "micro-agents" — disposable, single-purpose workflows.
**Mechanism:** Trigger = inbound email matching a sender or pattern. Reads = the email body (extracts a proposed time), the user's calendar. Acts = sends a calendar invite back if the slot is free, otherwise drafts a reply suggesting alternatives. Writes to = mailbox + calendar. Replaces = the 90 seconds of swivel-chair work between Outlook and Calendar that happens 5–15 times a day for any meeting-driven role.
**Why agentic:** The trigger is an external event (email arrival), the action is autonomous (booking without a human in the loop on the green path), and the agent disappears once the task is done. There is no UI to look at — the only visible artifact is the invite that lands in the requester's inbox. The interesting move is treating agents as cheap and disposable rather than as a single "assistant" you over-build.
**SMB translation:** Vertical = independent college counselors (Jenn). Trigger = inbound parent email containing a meeting-request phrase ("can we set up a call", "are you free Tuesday at 4"). Stack = Gmail push notifications → n8n or Make webhook → Claude API for time extraction and intent classification → Google Calendar API for availability + invite creation. Maybe $40/mo in tooling on top of her existing Workspace. Role replaced = Jenn doing manual Gmail-to-Calendar reconciliation 8–12 times a day during peak season. Pitch sentence: *Every parent meeting request books itself before you've finished your coffee — and the ones it can't book come back to you with three pre-checked alternatives ready to send.*

### 2. The tiered agent fleet that closes small deals end-to-end and tags out for big ones — `caped`, `music-space`
**Source:** [Lenny's Podcast — "We replaced our sales team with 20 AI agents"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/we-replaced-our-sales-team-with-20-ai-agents) with Jason Lemkin (SaaStr). Lemkin runs 20 named agents managed by 1.2 humans. Key mechanism detail: low-ticket sponsorship sales now run *entirely* through AI — discovery, qualification, pricing, contract — with humans only re-entering at procurement. High-ticket deals get a hard handoff at the negotiation stage. SaaStr is sending 70k personalized emails vs. 7k pre-AI, and an agent closed a sponsorship at 11pm on a Saturday.
**Mechanism:** Trigger = inbound lead (form fill, podcast subscriber crossing a threshold, opt-in click). Reads = lead's company, prior interactions, segment fit. Acts = the agent owns the *full cycle* up to a configurable deal-size or deal-type ceiling. Below the ceiling: agent emails, qualifies, prices, sends the contract, escalates only on procurement/legal flags. Above the ceiling: agent does discovery and brief, then hands a packaged deal to a human for negotiation. Replaces = the entire low-ticket SDR/AE workflow plus most of the prep work for high-ticket deals.
**Why agentic:** This isn't a chatbot routing tickets — the agent is closing revenue without a human approval step on the green path for sub-threshold deals. The interesting design choice is the *deal-size threshold as a trigger boundary*, not a confidence score or a human-review queue. Either the agent owns it or it doesn't.
**SMB translation:** Vertical = Capital Educators (SAT/ACT prep) and The Music Space (lesson studio). Both have a low-ticket / high-ticket split that nobody is exploiting:
- **CapEd:** trial diagnostic + single-test boot camp = low-ticket, runs end-to-end through agent (intake form → diagnostic schedule → score-report follow-up → boot-camp upsell email → Stripe checkout). Multi-month tutoring packages = high-ticket, agent does discovery and prep, Debbie takes the close call.
- **Music Space:** trial lesson + single-month month-to-month = low-ticket, agent owns it. Semester contracts and recital-prep packages = high-ticket, Justin takes the parent call.

Stack = Squarespace/Calendly/Opus1 webhooks → n8n → Claude with a deal-size router as the first node → Stripe + DocuSign for the small tier, calendar handoff to owner for the big tier. ~$200–400/mo. Role replaced = the owner answering every inbound the same way regardless of deal size, and missing nights/weekends. Pitch sentence: *Trials and single-package signups close themselves end-to-end at midnight on Sunday. Your time goes to the families considering a $4,000 commitment, not the ones already swiping the card.*

### 3. The vertical agent that converts messy domain artifacts into structured deliverables — `jenn`
**Source:** [Avoice (YC W26)](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/PQC-avoice-the-ai-workspace-for-architects) — "AI workspace for architects." Firms with $300M+ in active projects on the platform. The mechanism is deliberately *not* a chatbot or a search box.
**Mechanism:** Trigger = a project artifact lands in the workspace (a spec PDF, a marked-up drawing, a contract redline, a code update). Reads = the firm's full corpus of past projects, current project files, applicable building codes, and prior firm-style standards. Acts = drafts the next required deliverable in the firm's voice — written specs, a compliance checklist with citations, a coordinated schedule, or a QA/QC summary. Writes to = the project workspace as an editable draft. Replaces = the junior architect doing 6–10 hours of cross-referencing and copy-paste from prior projects to produce a first draft.
**Why agentic:** The agent triggers on artifact arrival, not on a user query. It doesn't display project knowledge; it *writes* the deliverable the human would have written. The architect's only job is to edit the draft and approve. It's the difference between "search your past projects" (dashboard) and "your past projects already filled out the next document for you" (agent).
**SMB translation:** Vertical = Jenn (IECs). The exact same mechanic applies: counselors juggle messy artifacts per student (transcripts, essay drafts, recommender notes, school research, college lists, parent emails) and the work product is a series of structured documents (school list rationales, essay feedback memos, application timelines, parent updates). Trigger = a new artifact lands in the student's folder (Drive watcher) OR a milestone date hits (Cal trigger 14 days before deadline). Reads = the student's full folder + Jenn's archive of similar prior students (anonymized) + the school's published criteria. Acts = drafts the next deliverable — e.g., "School list rationale v3 incorporating the new SAT score" or "Parent update: where Maya stands with 17 days until ED deadline." Writes to = a Google Doc draft tagged "needs Jenn's review." Stack = Drive trigger → n8n → Claude with the firm's prior-student corpus as a retrieval index → Docs API. Probably $300–500/mo per counselor at her volume. Role replaced = the 6–10 hours/week Jenn spends generating boilerplate parent updates and rationale memos from scratch when 80% of the structure is already in her past work. Pitch sentence: *Your archive of past students stops being a graveyard and starts writing tomorrow's parent updates and school-list rationales for you — you just edit and send.*
