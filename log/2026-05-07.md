# 2026-05-07 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
The overnight property scouting agent has automated real estate research, scanning listings while you sleep and filtering by precise parameters. Same pattern works for any business that needs continuous market monitoring: contractors scouting supplier catalogs, restaurants tracking competitor menus, lawyers monitoring court filings.

## Patterns

### 1. The overnight property scout that works while you sleep
*(tags: `small-construction`, `restaurants`, `small-law-firms`, `accountants`)*

Kim Lewis Howard from Small Business Big AI documented two AI agents that scan commercial real estate listings across three Florida markets every morning, filtering for her exact parameters and surfacing move-in-ready options while she sleeps. The agents search property databases continuously, evaluate listings against predetermined criteria like size, location, price range, and zoning, flag properties that match specifications, and generate ranked lists with key details highlighted. When she needs expansion locations for her AAA insurance agency build, the agent works overnight to build a qualified roster while she focuses on business operations ([Small Business Big AI podcast, May 2026](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/small-business-big-ai/id1768659876)).

At a small construction company bidding commercial projects, the same mechanism triggers on cron schedule to monitor supplier catalogs for specialty materials like HVAC components or electrical fixtures. The agent scans distributor inventory systems, checks availability for specific part numbers, compares pricing across vendors, and alerts when critical materials drop below cost thresholds or come back in stock. It knows which suppliers offer bulk discounts, have reliable lead times, and carry the exact specifications needed for upcoming projects. Stack is supplier API access plus web scraping plus Claude for price analysis plus Slack notifications. Around $300-500 per month. Replaces the project manager spending hours weekly calling suppliers and checking inventory for materials that may not even be available. Pitch: you get first notification when critical materials become available at target pricing, with lead times confirmed before your competitors even know about it.

At a restaurant monitoring competitor menus and pricing, the agent tracks local restaurant websites, delivery platforms, and social media for menu changes, new items, and promotional pricing. It identifies trending ingredients, monitors portion sizes through image analysis, and tracks competitive pricing patterns across different dayparts. Around $200-400 per month for multi-location monitoring. Replaces the owner or manager spending time manually checking competitor offerings and pricing strategies.

### 2. The sales opportunity agent that builds itself
*(tags: `small-law-firms`, `accountants`, `consultants`, `medical-practices`)*

Rippling reported that one sales consultant built a sales opportunity agent with zero engineering support that now researches accounts, summarizes sales calls, and posts deal briefs automatically, saving reps five to six hours every week. The agent triggers on new inbound leads or scheduled follow-ups, searches company databases for account history and firmographics, analyzes meeting transcripts for key decision criteria and objections, drafts personalized follow-up emails with relevant case studies, and updates CRM records with next steps and timeline predictions ([OpenAI Workspace Agents, May 2026](https://openai.com/index/introducing-workspace-agents-in-chatgpt/)).

At a small law firm handling business litigation, the same mechanism triggers when new consultation requests come in through website forms or referral calls. The agent researches the prospect company through corporate filings and news searches, identifies key personnel and business challenges, analyzes case type against the firm's win rates and fee structures, drafts intake questions tailored to the specific legal issue, and prepares conflict checks against existing client database. It knows which types of cases generate higher fees, have stronger win probabilities, and align with partner expertise. Stack is intake form webhooks plus legal database APIs plus Claude for case analysis plus practice management system integration. Around $400-600 per month including platform costs. Replaces the paralegal spending 3 hours per new prospect researching background and preparing initial case evaluation. Pitch: every consultation arrives with complete background research, conflict checks completed, and customized intake questions ready, so you spend time on legal strategy instead of administrative preparation.

At an accounting practice managing tax preparation season, the agent handles initial client outreach, document collection, and return preparation workflows. It sends customized document request lists based on client type and prior year returns, follows up on missing documents with specific reminders, and pre-populates tax software with known client information. Around $300-500 per month during tax season. Replaces the staff accountant managing document collection and client communication workflows.

### 3. The supply chain agent workforce that thinks ahead
*(tags: `small-manufacturing`, `restaurants`, `retail`)*

Corvera, a YC W26 company, scaled from $0 to $33k MRR in four weeks with AI agents that automate CPG back-office operations, helping brands increase profits by up to 40% through automated order processing, inventory management, and demand forecasting. The agents parse email orders and PDFs, match them against product catalogs, execute fulfillment workflows, track inventory across multiple warehouses, sync stock levels between systems, and predict demand patterns based on sales history and seasonal trends ([YC W26, May 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/PES-corvera-ai-agents-for-cpg-brands)).

At a small manufacturing company producing custom components, the same mechanism triggers when new purchase orders arrive via email or EDI. The agent parses order specifications, checks material availability against current inventory, calculates production lead times based on machine capacity, generates work orders with detailed routing instructions, and updates delivery commitments with real-time status. It knows which materials have long lead times, which production slots are available, and when to escalate capacity constraints to management. Stack is order management system integration plus inventory APIs plus production planning software plus Claude for demand analysis. Around $800-1200 per month for multi-product manufacturers. Replaces the operations manager spending 15 hours weekly coordinating between sales, production, and procurement. Pitch: orders flow automatically from receipt to production scheduling, inventory stays perfectly synced, and you know exactly when delivery commitments are at risk before customers start calling.

At a restaurant chain managing food service distribution, the agent monitors supplier pricing, tracks ingredient availability, calculates menu cost impacts from commodity price changes, and automatically adjusts purchasing to maintain target food costs. It handles vendor negotiations, monitors delivery performance, and identifies substitution options when primary ingredients are unavailable. Around $400-700 per month for multi-location operations. Replaces the food service director managing supplier relationships and cost analysis across multiple locations.
