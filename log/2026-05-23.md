# 2026-05-23 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
Three concrete deployment patterns emerged today: multi-agent morning property scouting that replaces manual lead research, business knowledge mapping agents that replace consulting discovery phases, and skill-based AI companions that work across desktop applications to replace administrative coordination.

## Patterns

### 1. Morning Real Estate Scouting Agents
*(tags: `insurance`, `real-estate`, `property-management`, `small-construction`, `trades`)*

[Kim Lewis Howard from Small Business Big AI](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/small-business-big-ai/id1768659876) runs an AAA insurance agency with two AI agents that scan commercial real estate listings across three Florida markets every morning at 6 AM. Each agent is trained on her exact parameters—square footage ranges, location preferences, move-in requirements, price thresholds, and property types—then filters MLS feeds and commercial listing sites while she sleeps. By the time Kim starts her day, she has a prioritized list of viable properties with key details extracted, competitive analysis notes, and contact information ready for outreach.

This overnight scanning pattern could work across property-focused SMBs. For small construction companies, agents could scan permit databases and bid opportunities each morning, triggered by municipal website updates, reading project specifications and budget ranges, then creating prioritized lead lists with contact details and project timelines. The pitch: "Instead of spending two hours every morning hunting for leads, you wake up to qualified opportunities that match your capabilities and schedule." For property management companies, the same trigger could scan rental market listings, eviction records, and maintenance vendor availability, replacing the morning routine of manually checking multiple platforms to identify distressed properties for acquisition or management contract opportunities.

### 2. Business Process Documentation Agents
*(tags: `small-construction`, `restaurants`, `dental-and-vet-clinics`, `trades`, `accountants`)*

[Flowscope](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/ai) is a Y Combinator 2026 company running AI-native consulting that deploys on-site to learn how businesses actually operate. Their agents spend weeks embedded in client operations, documenting who touches what, where processes stall, what tasks are pure repetition, then redesigning and automating those workflows. The trigger is contract kickoff; agents monitor email threads, shadow phone calls, track document handoffs, and interview staff to map actual vs intended workflows. The output replaces the traditional discovery phase that consultants charge $50-200K for.

This embedded documentation pattern translates well to owner-operator businesses. For dental practices, agents could monitor scheduling calls, patient check-in flows, insurance verification steps, and billing handoffs for two weeks, triggered by practice onboarding. Using practice management software APIs, phone call transcripts via Twilio, and staff interview scheduling through Calendly, the agents would produce workflow maps and automation recommendations, replacing expensive practice management consultants. The pitch: "We'll spend two weeks learning exactly how your practice runs, then show you what can be automated for under $500/month instead of hiring another front-desk person." Similar deployment works for restaurants tracking order-to-kitchen workflows, HVAC companies mapping job dispatch to completion, or accounting firms analyzing client onboarding processes.

### 3. Desktop Skill Orchestration Agents
*(tags: `professional-services`, `accountants`, `home-health`, `restaurants`, `small-construction`)*

[OpenClaw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47296664) enables a one-person company running on Gemini's free tier through four specialized agents with distinct skill sets—scheduling, financial analysis, development, and operations—coordinated by 25 systemd timers that trigger different agents based on calendar events, email patterns, and file changes. Each agent has access to specific desktop applications and APIs, with scheduling agents handling meeting coordination through Google Calendar, financial agents processing invoices through QuickBooks integration, and operations agents managing project status through various tools. The system replaces the coordination overhead that typically requires hiring multiple part-time assistants.

Professional service firms could deploy similar skill-based orchestration. For accounting practices, agents could trigger based on client email types—tax prep emails activate preparation agents that pull client documents from Google Drive, pre-fill forms, and schedule review meetings, while audit inquiry emails trigger research agents that pull transaction histories and generate response drafts. The stack would integrate Google Workspace, practice management software like Drake or Lacerte, and phone scheduling through Calendly, replacing junior staff coordination tasks. The pitch: "Your agents handle the setup work so you only touch client files when they're ready for your expertise." The same pattern works for home health agencies where scheduling agents coordinate aide availability with patient needs, or restaurants where inventory agents trigger reorders based on POS data and kitchen prep schedules.
