# 2026-08-05 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
Four concrete patterns today, all built around external-signal triggers rather than user prompts: weather forecasts rescheduling roofing crews, calendar dates opening month-end close checklists, contract renewal windows generating pre-populated negotiation briefs, and shopper queries dispatching swarms of outbound phone calls to local stores. The common thread is the trigger class — a schedule, a data feed, or a portfolio event moves before anyone opens their laptop, and the agent has already read context and produced a draft by the time a human touches it.

## Patterns

### 1. Weather-triggered field crew rescheduling agent
Source: RoofClaw / Adam Sand on AI & Home Services podcast (https://www.rooferscoffeeshop.com/webinar/roofclaw-your-ai-powered-chief-of-staff) — a three-location storm restoration roofer replaced a full-time employee whose sole job was checking the weather and rescheduling crews. That role was paid roughly six figures. The RoofClaw agent runs on a Mac Mini on the office desk, hooked into HubSpot, JustCall, and Zuper, and now handles it for about three dollars a day. The displaced employee moved into production management as the company opened a fourth location.
Mechanism: trigger is a forecast update or a weather alert crossing a rule threshold (wind speed, precipitation probability, hail risk within a zip code radius). Agent reads today and tomorrow's job schedule from Zuper, crew and truck assignments, customer contact list, and the current forecast for each job site. It computes which jobs are still viable, drafts SMS or email rescheduling messages through JustCall for the affected customers with two or three alternate slots, updates the job status in Zuper, and posts a summary of the reshuffled day into HubSpot. Owner opens their laptop to a rescheduled schedule, not a weather app.
Why agentic: the pattern replaces a dashboard-plus-human loop (check weather, cross-reference schedule, decide, call customers, update CRM) with an autonomous rule-driven loop that ends in customer-facing messages and a mutated schedule.
Generalizable core: external data feed crosses a threshold → agent reads calendar or work queue + affected records → drafts and sends customer communications + mutates the underlying system of record.
Deployability: local hardware plus API glue to CRM, scheduling, and comms (HubSpot, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, Zuper, Twilio, JustCall). A week to two weeks of build once the rescheduling rules are pinned down. Same skeleton lands anywhere a work schedule is exposed to weather, traffic, supplier delivery slips, or supply chain disruption — landscapers, pool service, paving, delivery, outdoor events, film production. Replaces a scheduler or dispatcher's morning triage.

### 2. Contract renewal briefing agent
Source: Ironclad Renewal Agent, launched May 15, 2026 (https://support.ironcladapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/40675593597207-What-s-New-in-Ironclad-June-2026) — free trial for all Ironclad Editions customers through Sept 1, then paid via AI Credits. Aimed at procurement teams whose vendor contracts silently auto-renewed because nobody caught the window in time.
Mechanism: trigger is a contract entering a staged renewal window (120, 90, 30, and 7 days before expiration) as classified from parsed contract metadata. Agent reads the contract text, historical spend, usage or seat data if connected, notes from prior negotiations, and vendor health signals. It produces a Renewal Brief that summarizes what the contract covers and how it is used, names the likely internal owner, flags cost savings the Cost Savings Agent has identified (expiring discounts, underused minimums, pricing anomalies), and lists vendor risk indicators. The brief lands in the Vendor Renewals dashboard; the procurement lead clicks through and the agent kicks off the appropriate renewal workflow, routing to legal or the internal business owner.
Why agentic: the passive version of this is a renewal calendar (a dashboard). The agent version writes the negotiation prep, identifies who owns the vendor, surfaces the leverage points, and then triggers the downstream workflow. Procurement stops discovering renewals during audits and starts opening pre-briefed negotiations.
Generalizable core: portfolio timeline event crosses a staged threshold → agent reads the record + adjacent context (usage, spend, related communications) → drafts a prep brief + routes work to the accountable human.
Deployability: hosted for teams already on Ironclad, DocuSign CLM, Icertis, or Conga; roughly a month for a bespoke build on top of a shared drive + spend export + Claude with an MCP contract-store. The pattern generalizes hard: any timeline-bounded portfolio a business owns can drive it — insurance policies at renewal, service contracts at expiration, patents at maintenance-fee windows, leases before rent-review dates, warranties at expiration. Replaces the analyst who used to hunt spreadsheets for what is expiring soon.

### 3. Month-end close draft agent
Source: Anthropic Finance Agents, May 5, 2026 (https://www.anthropic.com/news/finance-agents) — ten ready-to-run templates including a Month-End Closer, distributed as plugins for Claude Cowork and Claude Code and as cookbooks for Claude Managed Agents. Goldman Sachs has confirmed production deployment (https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/47306/goldman-sachs-enlists-anthropic-for-accounting-and-compliance-ai-agents) focused on accounting for trades and transactions and on client vetting and onboarding.
Mechanism: trigger is calendar-based (final business day of the period, or the first working day of the new month). Agent reads the firm's close checklist, prior close reports as templates, general-ledger balances, sub-ledger reconciliation status, and supporting documents that landed in shared drives during the month. It runs the checklist item by item, prepares draft journal entries, flags variance anomalies against prior periods, and produces the close report as a draft. It writes nothing to the books of record and executes no transactions. A qualified human reviews, approves, and posts.
Why agentic: dashboards for a close show status. The agent produces the actual work product — the entries, the reconciliation, the report draft — and hands the reviewer a finished document instead of a to-do list. The controller becomes an approver, not a preparer.
Generalizable core: schedule fires → agent reads checklist + templates + system-of-record data + inbox artifacts → produces reviewable draft artifacts + queues them for human sign-off. Nothing writes to production until a human clicks.
Deployability: Anthropic's finance templates ship as plugins; roughly a week to configure to a specific chart of accounts and close checklist for a smaller shop. Larger firms are treating it as a month or two of integration onto their ERP. Same skeleton fits any period-end closing ritual: quarterly board reports, weekly ops reviews, month-end payroll, quarterly HR compliance filings, tax provisioning. Replaces two to five days of a controller or senior accountant's month.

### 4. Outbound phone-swarm information-gathering agent
Source: Google Search "Let Google Call" (https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/google-unleashes-shopping-ai-bots-that-call-stores-and-buy-for-you) — rolled out August 2026 for U.S. toys, health and beauty, and electronics. A shopper searches for a product "near me," picks the option, and Google's voice agent phones several local retailers in parallel to ask about stock, pricing, and hold options, then texts or emails a summary. The agent identifies itself as automated on each call.
Mechanism: trigger is a user intent captured through search. Agent reads the query, resolves the product SKU, and enumerates nearby physical retailers from local business data. It then places outbound phone calls in parallel using synthesized voice, follows a short script (does the store carry the item, in what quantity, what price, can it be held), captures the operator's responses, retries on hold and disconnect, and aggregates the results into a ranked summary that gets delivered by SMS or email.
Why agentic: this is the opposite of a dashboard — there is nothing static to look at. The agent's action is a real-world side effect (a phone call to a human) and the value is the aggregated result that pulls information out of systems no API can reach.
Generalizable core: user goal or trigger → agent enumerates a set of external human-answered endpoints → places outbound calls or messages in parallel with a scripted extraction goal → aggregates responses into a structured summary.
Deployability: the plumbing exists off the shelf now: Vapi or Retell or ElevenLabs Conversational for the voice layer, a local-business data source (Google Places, SerpAPI), and Claude to plan the script and merge the results. A week for a working prototype at a single business. The B2C direction is Google's; the B2B applications are large and mostly not built: sourcing agents calling suppliers to check part availability and lead times, staffing coordinators calling temp agencies for shift coverage, insurance adjusters calling body shops for repair estimates, wholesale buyers calling farms for produce availability, contractors calling subcontractors to confirm crew availability for next week. Replaces the phone-work portion of buyer, dispatcher, or sourcing roles.
