## Patterns

### 1. Competitor-intelligence agent that kills the tracking spreadsheet — `sollos` (also `caped`, `ls-custom`, `jenn`, `general`)
**Source:** [Jon Matzner on X, May 11 2026](https://x.com/MatznerJon/status/2053824192173502598) — Matzner runs Extreme Outsourcing and co-hosts Lazy Leverage with Peter Lohmann (residential property mgmt). The thread: a property manager had paid an assistant $1,000/mo for 12 years to manually track competitor vacancies, rent changes, days-on-market, and local supply trends across a maintained spreadsheet. His team replaced the human + spreadsheet with an agent.
**Mechanism:** Scheduled trigger (daily / pre-leasing-meeting) → agent reads a fixed list of competitor listing URLs and rental marketplace pages → extracts current rent, square footage, beds/baths, vacancy status, days listed, concessions → diffs against yesterday's snapshot → writes new rows to a structured table and posts a Slack/email summary highlighting (a) price changes, (b) new vacancies, (c) units that have been sitting >30 days. Replaces a recurring human task that was being treated as a perpetual line item.
**Why agentic:** It is not a dashboard fed by an API. There is no API for "what is my competitor charging today." The agent has to navigate inconsistent listing pages, infer when a unit is the same unit week-over-week, decide what changed, and route the delta into a decision-ready summary. The output is a daily diff and a recommended action ("Building B dropped rent 4%, two units now vacant"), not a chart.
**SMB translation:**
- **Vertical:** Sollos yerba mate (CPG). Stephen has no real-time view of how the three other small-batch yerba brands (and the established Argentina imports) are pricing, promoting, or stocking at the retail edge. Same problem as Matzner's property manager: the data exists in public web pages, but nobody on a two-person team has time to harvest it.
- **Trigger:** Cron at 6 AM ET. Optional second trigger: any time Stephen tags a SKU in Notion as "under pricing review."
- **Stack:** Claude API with web browsing + a small Postgres or Airtable as the snapshot store; n8n or a small Next.js worker to schedule and post results to Slack/email. List of monitored URLs = ~20 (Amazon SKU pages for each competitor, each competitor's DTC shop, a handful of regional grocery e-commerce sites like Fresh Direct / Sprouts online / Whole Foods online). Optional: Apify or Bright Data if anti-bot becomes an issue. All-in under $200/mo.
- **Role replaced or augmented:** The VA or intern Stephen would otherwise hire for competitive pricing reviews. Also replaces the "I should build a dashboard for this" instinct — he doesn't need to *look* at competitor data, he needs to be *told* when it moves.
- **Pitch sentence:** "Every morning before you open your laptop you get a one-screen note that says 'Mate Factor dropped to $14.99 on Amazon, Yerbaé added a 20% promo on their DTC site, Guayaki is out of stock on the 16-pack at Whole Foods' — instead of a dashboard you never check."
- **Secondary translations:** **Caped** (Capital Educators) → same pattern pointed at competitor SAT/ACT prep companies' published pricing, package changes, free-trial offers, and instructor counts across their websites and Google reviews; output is a weekly competitive note for Debbie before her marketing meeting. **LS Custom / CollegePlannerPro / GradBetter** → for any IEC tracking competitor counselors, the agent watches competitors' published packages, fees, sample student outcomes, and blog/social cadence and flags meaningful changes. **Jenn directly** → smaller-scale version pointed at the three or four IECs in her metro plus a handful of national premium counselors (LS Custom, Collegewise) — she gets a monthly "what your competitors changed" brief without ever logging into anyone's site.

### 2. Pre-meeting brief synthesizer — `jenn` (also `caped`, `general`)
**Source:** [Anthropic — Claude finance agent templates, May 5 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/finance-agents). The launch ships ten ready-to-run agent templates for financial services. Most of them duplicate things already in the log (month-end close, KYC, etc. → see 5/25 invoice chase entry), but one is genuinely a different shape: the **Meeting Preparer** agent. It "assembles briefs before calls," pulling from filings, prior call notes, CRM, and recent news so the banker walks in with a fresh, synthesized one-pager.
**Mechanism:** Calendar trigger fires 60–90 minutes before a scheduled client meeting → agent reads (a) the client's CRM record, (b) the last 2–3 meeting notes / email threads with that client, (c) any new documents tied to that client (filings, model updates, new transcripts), (d) any flagged news → produces a structured brief with sections: status since last meeting, open questions, recent changes, suggested talking points → delivers as an email + a doc dropped in a shared folder. Replaces the 30–60 minutes a senior person spends re-reading files before each call, or the silent reality that most calls happen un-prepped.
**Why agentic:** The trigger is an event in the world (calendar), not a request from the user. The agent decides what to read based on who the meeting is with, summarizes across heterogeneous sources, and produces a deliverable. A dashboard cannot do this — the brief needs to be different for every client and every meeting and assembled at the right moment.
**SMB translation:**
- **Vertical:** Jenn (IEC). She runs back-to-back 30–60 min student/parent meetings during application season. Today she opens CommonApp, her own student notes doc, and her last email thread with that family — for every single meeting — and tries to remember where things stood.
- **Trigger:** Google Calendar event with a student's name in the title, 60 minutes before start.
- **Stack:** Claude API + Google Calendar MCP + Google Drive MCP + Gmail MCP. Reads (a) the student folder in Drive (essay drafts, school list, prior session notes), (b) any email threads tagged for that student, (c) optional CommonApp portal scrape or manual status notes Jenn maintains. Writes a one-page brief into Drive and emails her a link. Could run on Lindy or n8n; we could also wrap it in a tiny Next.js app for her so the brief shows up in a single browser tab she opens at the meeting.
- **Role replaced or augmented:** The "rummage through five tabs for ten minutes before each meeting" task. Replaces what an associate counselor would do in a larger practice. Augments Jenn's memory — she sees the same student every two weeks and meaningful state changes between meetings get lost.
- **Pitch sentence:** "Sixty minutes before every parent meeting, you get a one-pager that says: 'Maya — finished Common App essay v3 on Tuesday, mom emailed about USC Marshall on Wednesday, Brown supplement still not started, you said last session you'd nudge her on UMich by 5/30 — open question.' Instead of opening five tabs, you open one email."
- **Secondary translation:** **Caped** → same pattern triggered before each SAT/ACT tutoring session. Brief synthesizes (a) last session's homework assignment vs what the student actually completed, (b) most recent practice test section scores and which sub-topics regressed, (c) any parent communications in the last week, (d) which problem types are next in the curriculum. Replaces the 5–10 min the tutor spends preparing per session. Pitch: "Your tutors walk into every session already knowing Maya bombed Q23–Q31 on Reading last Tuesday and her mom emailed about test anxiety on Friday."
