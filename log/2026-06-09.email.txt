Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-06-09 — 4 patterns | in-pocket technician coaching for trades

Four patterns this cycle, after an 11-day gap since the last successful scan on 5/28. The most copyable is Robby's in-pocket technician coaching loop for home-services trades, which turns each scheduled visit into a real-time upsell surface and writes follow-up quotes back to the field service system. The returns-recovery and conference/RFP scout patterns extend the playbook into supplier reconciliation and expertise-business outbound, neither of which has been logged before.

1) In-pocket technician coaching that turns every service call into an upsell loop
Robby (tryrobby.com, YC W26) is selling to HVAC, plumbing, and electrical shops with 5-50 techs. The trigger is a tech opening a scheduled job in ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber. The agent pulls prior equipment records, warranty status, age of unit, and what neighbors have bought, then pushes a pre-visit brief to the tech's phone with two or three talking points. During the visit the tech taps "saw it" or "didn't apply" and the agent drafts a follow-up quote in the FSM the moment the visit closes. Replaces the GM's end-of-day review of every tech's notes plus the tech's own paperwork.

Same loop drops into pest control on FieldRoutes (trigger: recurring quarterly stop; agent surfaces expiring annual contracts and neighbor termite bonds), pool service on Skimmer (equipment age, chemical-use trends), and appliance repair shops on ServiceFusion (warranty-period tickets cross-referenced with manufacturer recall feeds). Stack is the FSM mobile app + Claude with vision for equipment photos + a lookup of county property data. The pitch: your techs walk in already knowing what to sell, and the quote is in the customer's inbox before they finish washing their hands.

2) The med-legal case operator that replaces a paralegal seat
Wayco (wayco.ai, YC W26) is shipping to personal-injury law firms in the 5-40 attorney range. The trigger is a new case intake — web form, transferred call, or referral by email or fax. The agent reads the intake form, scanned medical records, accident report, and prior provider history, then matches the claimant to a treating provider in the firm's referral network, builds a medical chronology, and drafts the record-review summary. Output lands in Filevine, CloudLex, or Litify, with a referral packet sent to the provider and a Slack ping to the case attorney. Replaces a $50-80K paralegal seat per ~150 active cases.

Copies into workers' comp boutiques (trigger: injury intake call; writes to Smokeball or PracticePanther), SSDI/disability firms (higher case volume, same chronology loop), and any specialty where case value depends on getting records summarized fast. Stack is the case management system + HIPAA-compliant document parsing + Claude for synthesis + Twilio or fax-to-email on the inbound side. Pitch: stop paying $70K to chronicle records, and stop losing two weeks at the front of every case while it sits in a paralegal queue.

3) The opportunity-scout that drafts your next speaking proposal
Pragmatic Digital, a two-person AI consultancy, has a Lindy agent that runs weekly to scan open speaker-call pages, industry conference sites, and event aggregators (Lindy case study: lindy.ai/case-study/pragmatic-transformed-their-consulting). The agent filters against their target persona, and on a hit pulls the call-for-speakers form plus their library of past proposals, then drafts a full submission in their voice — title, abstract, bio, fit narrative — for inbox review. What used to be a 1.5-week senior-partner cycle is now twenty minutes of scout time and under two days to a finished proposal.

Fits any SMB whose growth depends on getting picked from a public list. A boutique law firm chasing government work runs it against SAM.gov, BidNet, and state procurement portals filtered by NAICS code, drafting capability statements from past wins. A wedding photographer runs it against HoneyBook, The Knot, and local venue partner pages for new preferred-vendor openings. A CPE-credentialed accounting trainer scans state-bar and AICPA event pages and drafts session proposals matched to their published topics. Stack is Lindy or n8n + Claude + Google Drive APIs, maybe $200/month. Pitch: stop missing RFPs and stop letting speaking slots default to the firms with a full-time BD person.

4) The supplier-side returns chaser that recovers money small operators usually eat
Nicole Ruiz demonstrated the personal version on the How I AI podcast in early June (lennysnewsletter.com/p/shopping-with-claude) — Claude as a household returns concierge that reads her inbox for the original order, checks the merchant's return policy, drafts the RMA with the correct policy citation and proof attached, and tracks the credit. SMB version triggers on a receiving flag: damaged-pallet photo, short-shipment line on a delivery invoice, defective part. Agent pulls the PO from the supplier portal, drafts the credit request, files it, and logs the expected credit. Replaces the 15-30 minutes per item of "find the receipt, look up the policy, write the email, chase the credit" — work most SMB owners simply don't do because it isn't worth their hour.

A 12-seat cafe using Sysco or US Foods runs this on every short-ship (n8n + portal scrape + Claude + Gmail, ~$80/month all-in). A two-bay auto shop running RockAuto and WorldPac runs the same loop on every core return. A small Shopify operation triggers on Stripe refunds and files the corresponding supplier-side credit. Pitch to a restaurant owner: every short-shipped case of romaine and every wrong-vintage wine bottle gets a credit filed before service starts, instead of getting eaten at the end of the month.
