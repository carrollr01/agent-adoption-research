Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-05-16 — 3 patterns | brokerage agents running on Mac Minis

https://github.com/carrollr01/agent-adoption-research/blob/claude/epic-fermat-8J8kM/log/2026-05-16.md

Three concrete patterns today, anchored by Panta, a YC W26 commercial insurance brokerage where the back-office layer is agents running computer-use against the same carrier portals a human broker would click through.

The brokerage agent that does the office's clicking and waiting. Panta's agents run on a rack of Mac Minis and operate inside the actual carrier portals, Outlook, softphones, and PDFs that a licensed broker uses. Trigger is an inbound submission with loss runs attached. The agent picks the right carriers for the risk, fills ACORDs and market apps in each portal, chases underwriters by email and phone until quotes are bindable, and surfaces them for a licensed human to sign off. The new angle vs. prior voice-broker and CRM-agency entries is that the agent's day is mostly clicking, attaching, and waiting inside somebody else's regulated software. Cleanest copy is a small construction company that does its own permit work — the agent files at the city portal, attaches the right PDFs, and chases the plan reviewer. Around $800-1,200 per month per contractor running 8-15 active permits. Same shape works for a medical-billing shop appealing denied claims inside Availity, UHC, Aetna, and Anthem portals.

The POS-driven ordering agent. Spring 2026 was the moment every restaurant-POS vendor shipped an agent — Square with MarketMan, Toast, Thanx. Trigger is the close-of-day POS feed. The agent decrements line-item sales against the recipe map to estimate true ingredient depletion, compares against on-hand and the next 5-7 days of pattern, drafts purchase orders against preferred vendors, and queues them for a one-tap morning approval. The under-served copy is the independent grocery or bodega running on Square Retail — same trigger, same draft-PO action, $300-500 per month per location. Replaces the owner doing reorder math at 11pm Sunday. Same shape works for a coffee shop on Toast or Shopify POS ordering green beans and milk.

The maintenance-triage agent that dispatches a vendor before the property manager logs in. A housing-cooperative deployment is handling 76% of inbound tenant calls end-to-end, average handle time dropping from 8 minutes manual to 3 minutes 10 seconds. Trigger is a tenant call or text. The voice agent asks structured diagnostic questions, classifies into emergency/urgent/routine, opens a work order in Buildium or AppFolio, picks the right pre-approved vendor for the trade, dispatches them with a window, and texts the tenant the confirmation. The new angle is the triage + dispatch combination — prior logs covered intake voice agents that book the next slot, this one decides priority, picks the vendor, and assigns the job. Cleanest copy is the small dental or vet practice running after-hours triage. Trigger: the after-hours call. Agent classifies acuity, pulls the chart from Dentrix, Eaglesoft, or AVImark, and either books the morning, escalates to the on-call cell, or routes to the nearest emergency clinic with chart summary attached. Around $400-700 per month for a 2-4 provider practice. Replaces the answering service that just takes a message at $1 per call.

Through-line: agents are starting to live inside the SaaS the operator already pays for — the carrier portal, the POS, the property-management system — and act there, rather than being a separate dashboard the operator has to open.
