Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-06-13 — 4 patterns: fleet breakdown orchestration, construction loan draws, concrete takeoff, RFP first-draft

https://github.com/carrollr01/agent-adoption-research/blob/claude/determined-meitner-ph363g/log/2026-06-13.md

Four patterns today, all clustered in capital-intensive, document-heavy verticals that historically resisted automation. The standout is Carma's Neo agent. When a truck breaks down, gets flagged for an OEM recall, or sits idle past a threshold, Neo turns that signal into a fully built work order — vendor selected from a performance-scored network, labor routed, the resulting invoice audited against pricing benchmarks, all before a dispatcher would have finished returning the driver's call. Fortune 500 construction and disaster restoration fleets are running it on thousands of vehicles. The generalizable skeleton — telemetry signal from a physical asset, agent assembles history plus vendor network plus pricing benchmarks, dispatches the workflow, audits the invoice — fits any SMB fleet with real telematics: trucking, school buses, small utility contractors, food delivery operators with leased vans.

The second pattern is Built Technologies' Draw Agent for construction lending. A draw request comes in; the agent reads the inspection report (photos and narrative), checks lien waivers, validates percent complete against draw amount, and applies the lender's disbursement rules. Routine draws clear in three minutes versus the prior seven days. Exceptions get routed to a human reviewer with a specific flagged reason. This is the cleanest example of replacing a reviewer role — read documents, cross-check rules, make a call — that we've seen this month. The skeleton ports to community-bank construction loans, private rehab lenders, and even vendor milestone payments at a small GC.

The third is Rudus, just out of YC's P26 batch. An estimator uploads a PDF plan set; the agent auto-classifies each sheet (foundations, sections, footing schedules), routes each to a sheet-specific extraction pipeline, identifies every concrete element on the drawings, calculates quantities, and lays them into a priced bid that exports cleanly to HCSS HeavyBid or Sage or Excel. Twenty to forty hours of senior-estimator hand-tracing collapses into a couple hours of review. The pattern — domain-specific document set arrives, agent classifies and extracts to a priced output that drops into the operator's existing tool — is the most reusable template we've seen for any trade where the estimator hand-extracts numbers from drawings or specs. Electrical takeoffs, mechanical, landscape design, custom millwork — all the same shape.

The fourth is Field Aerospace's n8n proposal automation. A government solicitation document lands in their proposals folder; the workflow parses the requirements into structured JSON, queries their knowledge base for matching past-performance writeups and reusable sections, and assembles an 80% draft with a compliance matrix in Slack. Two weeks of four-person work becomes 25 minutes. The skeleton — inbound document defines requirements, agent parses and matches against an internal reusable-content library, drops a first draft plus coverage map for a human to finish — is probably the highest-leverage pattern of the four because it generalizes anywhere a firm responds to structured external documents at volume: SBA-set-aside RFPs for small contractors, nonprofit grant applications, vendor security questionnaires, insurance renewal questionnaires, B2B services proposals.

Today's overall signal: the agent-deployment frontier has decisively moved into verticals that get described as "behind on tech." Construction lending, concrete contracting, fleet maintenance, defense proposal writing — all places where the work is document- and exception-heavy and the operators have always told themselves "no software can do this." The Anthropic Agent SDK monthly credit program kicks off June 15 and Procore launched five prebuilt construction agents on May 21, both suggesting the platform layer is now actively pushing into these sectors rather than waiting for them to ask.
