Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-04-25 — 5 patterns | Property maintenance fanned out to every contractor at once

The interesting move today is parallel dispatch. Brickwise's property-management agent fans a maintenance ticket out to multiple contractors simultaneously instead of round-robining them. Same mechanism drops cleanly into landscaping subs, pest-control franchises, and any home-services owner whose Saturday is spent texting subs one at a time.

Five patterns from today's scan, all from YC W26 except where noted:

The maintenance ticket that goes to every contractor at once. Brickwise (YC F25) is running maintenance dispatch for UK landlords under the brand name Alice. Tenant texts the maintenance line with a photo of a leaking sink. Alice triages, pulls the property record, and instead of emailing one plumber and waiting a day, fans out simultaneous job offers to a panel of contractors who match the trade and area. First confirmation wins, others get a polite decline, the tenant gets an ETA in the same SMS thread. The load-bearing idea is parallel solicitation with auto-chase. At a small landscaping company subbing tree work or irrigation, the agent reads a Jobber work-order tagged "needs sub," texts the top three subs at once with scope and rate cap, books the first yes. Around $150 a month. Replaces the owner texting subs from his truck. At a pest-control franchise during termite season, same mechanism handles route overflow to vetted independents.

The PO that matches itself before it hits the ledger. Pollinate (YC W26) is selling three-way-match AP to food wholesalers and small CPG manufacturers. Supplier invoice lands in the AP inbox, agent extracts every line item, finds the matching PO and goods-receipt record, and reconciles. Matches post automatically; only variances above threshold reach a human. At a small CPG brand on Cin7 or NetSuite, the inbox is the trigger and the warehouse manager's receiving log is the third record. Around $200 a month. Replaces the bookkeeper's Friday afternoon. Same mechanism works at a small specialty contractor reconciling supply-house invoices against project POs in QuickBooks.

The retailer email that becomes a confirmed sales order. Corvera (YC W26) hit $33K MRR with twelve brands inside their first month. The retailer emails a PO — PDF, email, sometimes a photo — to the brand's orders inbox. Agent parses it, validates SKUs against live inventory across warehouses, generates the sales order, and pings ops if anything can't ship complete. This is the mirror image of an AP agent and it's painful at small-brand scale because every retailer formats POs differently. At a 10-SKU CPG brand selling into a hundred regional accounts on Shopify Wholesale, Faire, and direct emails, the agent ingests, validates, generates the SO, and replies with confirmation and ship date. Around $250 a month. Replaces the founder re-keying retailer POs at 9 PM.

The compliance reviewer that reads every ad before it ships. Veriad (YC W26) is selling an AI compliance officer for marketing teams in regulated industries. New ad creative or landing page gets uploaded, agent reads it line by line against the brand's policy doc, the regulator's guidance (FTC, FDA, FINRA, state-level cannabis rules), and prior approved/rejected work. Returns redlines with cited rules. At a med spa running Meta and Google ads, the agent triggers when a creative lands in the marketing Drive folder and checks against FTC endorsement rules and the state medical board's advertising guidelines. Around $200 a month. Replaces the practice manager forwarding ads to a compliance lawyer at $400 an hour. Also fits financial planners under FINRA, and dispensaries navigating moving state rules.

The drone that moves the herd without a ranch hand. GrazeMate (YC W26) raised $1.2M to deploy fully autonomous mustering drones on Australian cattle stations. Rancher taps a button — "move the mob from paddock A to paddock B." Drones auto-position, watch animal posture through a reinforcement-learning model trained on livestock behavior (head down means calm, head up plus run means stress), apply pressure, back off when the herd shows distress. Only physical-world agent in our running log; useful reminder that "agentic" doesn't have to mean "in a Slack channel." Adjacent SMB pitches: feedlots moving cattle between pens, dairies moving the milking string. Translation cost is real — hardware, training data, regs — but the right pattern to know exists when an ag prospect describes a chronic labor shortage.

Pattern 1 (Brickwise) is the highest-leverage copy on this list because it's a software-only build and the parallel-dispatch trigger maps cleanly to anything where the owner currently does serial outreach by hand.
