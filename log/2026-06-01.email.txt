Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-06-01 — 3 patterns | AI broker calls for owner-operator truckers

Repo: https://github.com/carrollr01/agent-adoption-research/blob/claude/epic-fermat-3bysl/log/2026-06-01.md

Three concrete patterns today, all built around photos or phone lines as the trigger. The strongest is an AI agent that calls freight brokers on behalf of independent truckers, negotiates spot-market rates with real-time data, and reads back loads for the operator to approve. The same "your agent calls them, not you" shape is what makes the freight pattern translate cleanly into construction subcontracting, restaurant supplier sourcing, and clinic last-minute coverage.

1. AI broker calls for independent truckers
(tags: trucking, small-construction, restaurants, home-health, dental-and-vet-clinics)

Numeo (https://numeo.ai/spot), Owner Operator AI, and TruckSmarter Dispatch have pushed agentic freight dispatch into the owner-operator tier this spring. Numeo's pitch is direct: the agent calls brokers on your behalf, gathers the load details, and negotiates against the rate you've set as your floor, then hands back a one-screen summary for you to approve. Pricing starts at $0 for the Lite Chrome extension that sits inside DAT, with rate negotiation unlocking at $99/month. The trigger is a new load posted in DAT that matches the carrier's lane preferences. The agent dials the broker, holds a real phone conversation, queries real-time market data on what that lane should pay, and pushes for a number above the driver's minimum. TruckSmarter's beta drivers saved over 1,000 hours in a single week of broker calls. The replaced task is dialing 30 brokers a day from a rest stop and accepting whatever rate the first call-back will pay.

The mechanism that matters isn't voice — it's negotiation against a live market reference combined with outbound calls to a counterparty list the operator hasn't chosen. Drop that into small construction: a GC posts a need for a framing crew, and the GC's agent calls a list of subcontractors it knows from past jobs, reads them the scope, asks for a number, and reports back which two were under budget and available. Stack: BuilderTREND + Vapi for voice + Claude API. Role replaced: the office manager spending Wednesday afternoons on subcontractor calls. Pitch: "Your phone makes the dispatch calls, you decide which bid to take." Restaurants: when produce or protein costs spike, the chef's agent calls the three usual suppliers, gets verbal quotes, flags the cheapest reliable source. Dental and vet clinics: when a hygienist calls out, the agent dials a ranked list of fill-in contractors, confirms availability and rate, books the first yes.

2. Photo-to-estimate for roofing and exterior contractors
(tags: trades, small-construction, insurance, property-management)

SkyeBrowse and GAF QuickMeasure have made drone-plus-AI roof inspection a default homeowner ask in storm-affected markets this year. Trigger is a photo or short drone video uploaded into a contractor's inspection app, usually from a tech holding a phone or flying a $1,200 Mavic. The agent classifies damage types automatically — cracked shingles, missing flashing, ponding water, membrane blistering — each defect GPS-tagged and measured against a roof outline pulled from satellite imagery. From that the system produces square-footage measurements, a material list, and a labor estimate in roughly two minutes. Replaced task: a 30–45 minute site visit, a tape measure, and an estimator translating notes into a number back at the office. Cost is $150–$400 per inspection vs. $300–$600 manual. Large roofing companies are adopting this at twice the rate of small ones, which is exactly the gap A3 Dev exists to close.

The pattern generalizes anywhere a contractor or claims-handler currently sends a human to look at a thing. Insurance carriers have already pushed it into auto claims (24-hour turnaround vs 5–7 days for 78% of claims); the SMB version where the contractor runs the agent — not the carrier — so they can quote same-day hasn't happened yet. Property management: tenant uploads move-out photos through the existing portal, an agent compares to the move-in photo set, flags damage beyond normal wear, drafts the deposit-deduction line items with photos attached. Stack: AppFolio or Buildium + Claude with vision + a Google Doc template. Pitch: "Send the tenant photos through your existing portal, get a draft itemized deposit return before the office opens." Used-car dealers: a porter walks the trade-in lot with a phone, the agent flags reconditioning needs and posts a budget into the DMS before the appraiser walks back inside.

3. Photo invoice in, supplier reorder out
(tags: restaurants, dental-and-vet-clinics, home-health, small-construction, salons-and-spas)

Square Restaurant Inventory by MarketMan (https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-restaurant-inventory-marketman) launched globally April 2 and is the cleanest SMB version of this we've seen. A line cook snaps a photo of a paper supplier invoice. The agent parses line items, updates ingredient costs, reconciles the delivery against the standing PO, and pushes new unit costs through to recipe-level COGS in the Square POS. The same system watches usage against the POS sales feed, flags variance that indicates waste or theft, and either drafts the next PO for the chef to approve or fires it directly when stock crosses a threshold. $99/month per location on top of Square Plus. Replaced task: the back-office routine of pinning paper invoices to a clipboard, hand-keying on Sunday night, and noticing two weeks later that ribeye COGS quietly drifted from 32% to 41%.

The shape — phone photo of a vendor document, parse, reconcile, reorder — is sitting under-used in every SMB that takes paper deliveries. Dental and vet practices: receiving currently means an assistant ticks off a paper packing slip, the office manager keys invoices into QuickBooks later that week. An agent triggered by an inbox photo updates inventory in Dentrix or Cornerstone, reconciles against the PO, and warns when consumable burn rate jumps — useful for catching over-ordering and for spotting a hygienist quietly using twice as much composite as her colleagues. Pitch: "Photograph the packing slip; we keep your supply costs current and tell you when something looks off." Home health: same problem with medical supplies and DME, made worse because deliveries arrive at patient homes and the aide has the phone. Small construction: superintendent photos the delivery slip from the lumberyard, agent flags the missing 2x10s before the framing crew arrives Monday. Salons and spas: color, retail product, towels — the owner has been doing it by spreadsheet since 2008.
