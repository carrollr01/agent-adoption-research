Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-05-03 — 3 patterns | Cancellation backfill agent for clinics

https://github.com/RCarroll6/agent-adoption-research/blob/claude/epic-fermat-oSkY2/log/2026-05-03.md

The strongest pattern this week is the cancellation refill loop running at independent clinics through Solum Health (their agent is named Annie) and Novoflow. The trigger is the cancellation event in the practice management system. The agent reads the open slot, ranks the waitlist, places an outbound voice call to the top patient, books them in the EHR, and removes them from the list. If the first call doesn't connect, it walks the list. Solum reports filling around 70% of same-day cancellations within two hours, against the MGMA benchmark of 25-30% for manual phone work. Most front desks don't even treat a cancellation as a workflow today — it's a hole in the day. The agent treats it as a job with a clock attached.

This is the highest-leverage copy in healthcare-adjacent SMB right now. It drops cleanly into independent dental and vet practices, but the sharper economics live in physical therapy and personal training, where 30-60 minute slots paid hourly turn into pure margin loss when they go unfilled. A 6-therapist PT clinic refilling 7-12 slots a week at $120-180 a visit pays the agent back in a week. Stack is the PMS API plus Vapi or Retell for voice, around $400-600 per provider per month.

The second pattern is Pollinate's three-way-match agent, out of YC W26. It watches the AP inbox, extracts every supplier invoice, and matches it against the PO and goods receipt inside the customer's ERP — posting if it reconciles, flagging exceptions if it doesn't. They're hitting 60-80% touchless processing. The pattern itself isn't new (Ramp, Tipalti, AvidXchange, ApprovalMax all do versions), but what changed this year is that the extraction-and-matching step is robust enough at SMB scale to run on top of QuickBooks Online or Xero with no NetSuite-tier ERP underneath. At a small GC running QBO and Buildertrend, the agent reads supplier invoices arriving at ap@, matches them against open POs in Buildertrend, and posts to QBO with the right job-cost code. Replaces about a day a week of bookkeeper data entry.

The third pattern is the ad campaign auto-pause agent showing up across Gumloop and Make case studies in late April. Every 4-6 hours an agent reads CPA, conversion volume, and click costs by ad set, compares against owner-set thresholds, and acts: pauses the underperformers, scales the winners, texts the owner one line about what changed. The action is a Meta or Google Ads API write, not a dashboard alert. At a 10-truck HVAC shop running Google LSA and Meta lead-gen, the agent reads the prior day's cost-per-booked-job from ServiceTitan attribution and shifts spend before the budget burns for two more days. Around $150-250 a month, replaces the in-house marketing manager that 50-truck shops have and 10-truck shops don't.

The thread across all three: the trigger is an event the operator already considers part of their reality (a cancellation, a supplier invoice, an ad spend report), but the workflow that should follow either doesn't happen, happens too slowly, or only gets done by the founder at 9 PM Sunday.
