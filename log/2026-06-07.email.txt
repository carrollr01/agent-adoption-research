Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-06-07 — 5 patterns: EHR record-and-replay copies into title, freight, dental

https://github.com/carrollr01/agent-adoption-research/blob/claude/epic-fermat-mXLhE/log/2026-06-07.md

Five concrete patterns this week and a clear theme: agents are moving into the gnarly back-office work that lives behind legacy UIs and licensed-professional gatekeeping. The headline mechanism is Beacon Health's EHR record-and-replay primitive. A primary-care MA screen-records herself doing one chart workflow, and the agent re-runs it across thousands of patients on the actual EHR screen. No integration, no API, no five-figure interface kit. Beacon is live with an IPA covering 40,000 patients today. The same pattern copies into title insurance running county records into closing platforms, independent insurance agencies pulling renewals from carrier portals, and any vertical whose system of record is a 2008 web app that won't open up.

Lassie raised $35M on June 3 running adjacent territory inside dental and vet practices. The agent polls payer portals (Delta, MetLife, Cigna), pulls EOBs and remittance advice, matches each line to the right claim and patient in Dentrix or Open Dental, posts payments back into the PMS ledger, files appeals on denials, and only surfaces the 2% of exceptions worth a human. That's the insurance coordinator FTE in 700+ practices today, gone. The exact mechanism copies into auto body (CCC ONE), pharmacy reimbursements, PT, chiro, and optometry.

MochaCare, also YC W26, is the outbound voice flip side of the dental front-desk bots we've been logging. A caregiver calls out sick at 11 PM. The agent reads the roster, ranks candidates on geography, certification, hours-this-week, then parallel-calls and texts the top matches until one accepts. The agency owner sleeps through it. Same shape works for trucking driver no-shows, restaurant line cook call-outs, and substitute teacher pools.

Corvera (London, $4.2M raise, $33K MRR in four weeks, 12 CPG brands) cracks the email-PDF-to-ERP problem that Zapier flows have never been able to touch. A retailer sends a PO as a PDF, the agent parses it, matches line items against the brand's SKU catalogue ("12oz Cold Brew Original" resolves to "CB-OG-12"), generates the fulfillment order, queues the invoice, and only stops on ambiguous lines. The same shape applies to auto repair parts ordering, restaurant supply, and HVAC distributor invoices.

Last pattern, same week, two launches: General Legal (YC W26, $500 flat-fee contract review) and Superlegal (June 3, $117 per construction contract, first AI-owned firm under Utah's Sandbox). The agent does 80% of the work, a licensed professional reviews and signs off on 20%, sold as a flat-rate service instead of a SaaS seat. This is the model worth stealing for any licensure-gated vertical small software can't enter: tax prep with a CPA's signoff, dental prior auth, title search, immigration paperwork. The productizable angle for us isn't to start a law firm. It's to build the dispatcher that an existing CPA firm or insurance agency uses internally to run this model and stop turning down small jobs.

Sources mostly quiet outside Y Combinator. Reddit and Product Hunt blocked our fetches. X gave generic listicles. The signal this week was YC W26 launch posts plus one big Series A (Lassie) plus one regulatory-novelty law firm (Superlegal). Worth flagging that Ramp Stack's "watch one month-end close, then automate it for the rest of the year" onboarding primitive is the kind of pattern worth a future log when more case detail surfaces.
