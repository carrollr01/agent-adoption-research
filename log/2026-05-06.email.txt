# 2026-05-06 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
Two patterns today. (1) The on-site photo-to-quote agent that turns a phone snapshot into a customer-ready proposal in 60 seconds — direct fit for landscaping cold-outreach. (2) The cancellation-triggered waitlist agent that fills slots in the 15-minute window before patients move on — direct fit for Music Space, Jenn, and Caped.

## Patterns

### 1. The photo-to-quote agent that sends the proposal before you're back in the truck — `landscaping`, `hvac`, `pest-control`
**Source:** [BuildFolio AI Quote](https://build-folio.com/features/ai-quote/) and [Ravensight AI](https://ravensightai.com/) — two of several platforms now packaging this for trades; BuildFolio is shipping it on a $39/mo Pro plan covering 15+ trades including roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and landscaping. Ravensight starts at $199/mo with a "use the systems you already have" pitch.

**Mechanism:** Trigger is the contractor on site pulling out their phone and uploading a photo (or a stack of photos, or handwritten field notes, or audio walking through what they saw). A vision model parses the image for scope — square footage, plant types, equipment model, damage area, fixture count — then matches against the contractor's stored markup rules and pricing book. The agent generates an itemized line-item estimate, often in Good/Better/Best tiers, attaches the photo as part of the proposal, and sends an interactive proposal to the homeowner. Writes back to the CRM, the accounting system for invoice prep, and the contractor's job pipeline. Replaces the estimator going back to the office to type up a quote three days later — the lag that costs most contractors the job.

**Why agentic:** The photo is the trigger, not a UI. Once the contractor uploads, the agent runs a multi-step sequence: vision analysis → scope extraction → pricing lookup → markup application → tier generation → outbound delivery → CRM write. The contractor doesn't open another tab. A dashboard would show estimating activity; this writes the estimate.

**SMB translation — landscaping, our cold-outreach vertical:** Crew lead walks the property, takes 6 photos with the company app on their phone, taps "estimate." Within 60 seconds the homeowner receives a branded proposal with itemized line items: front lawn mowing area in sq ft, tree pruning hours by tree count, mulch volume by bed dimensions, irrigation head count from photo recognition. The lead is back at the truck before the customer has finished reading. Trigger is the in-app photo upload. Stack is the company's existing CRM (Jobber or Service Autopilot) plus a vision model (GPT-4o or Claude with vision via API) plus n8n or Make for the orchestration plus Twilio/email for delivery. Around $200-400 per month all-in for a small landscaping crew, well below the $39/job cost of an in-office estimator. Replaces the owner spending Friday nights pricing out the week's site visits, and replaces the 2-3 day quote turnaround that lets the next contractor in the door first. **Pitch:** every property visit becomes a sent quote before the customer has time to call your competitor.

### 2. The cancellation-triggered waitlist agent that beats the 15-minute window — `music-space`, `jenn`, `caped`
**Source:** [NexHealth Waitlist](https://www.nexhealth.com/features/waitlist), [Solv Experience OS with Maya](https://www.softwareadvice.com/medical/solv-profile/), and the [Doctible Easy Fill](https://www.doctible.com/features/easy-fill) feature — all shipping the same mechanism in 2026 for dental and medical practices. Practices using automated waitlist filling recover 60-80% of cancelled slots compared to 20-30% with manual calling. Fill rates peak when notifications reach patients within 15 minutes of the cancellation.

**Mechanism:** Trigger is a calendar cancellation event in the practice management system (or a no-show detected after a grace period). The agent reads the now-open slot, reads the waitlist filtered by provider, appointment type, urgency, and any patient-side scheduling constraints, ranks candidates by fit and recency, then sends a batched SMS with a one-click booking link to the top N candidates. First patient to tap the link gets booked into the PMS calendar; the agent then sends "thanks, slot filled" notifications to the others and updates their priority for the next opening. Cost runs $300-500/mo for a single-practice deployment.

**Why agentic:** The trigger isn't a person opening an app — it's a calendar state change. The work is a sequence (detect → rank → batch outreach → race-condition-resolve → write to PMS → notify losers), and the output is a booked appointment, not a report. A dashboard would tell the front desk "you have a cancellation, here's your waitlist." The agent fills the slot.

**SMB translation — Justin at Music Space:** Lesson studios live and die on schedule density. A 4pm guitar lesson cancels at 2pm, the teacher loses the slot, the studio loses the revenue. The trigger is the cancellation event in Opus1 (or whatever Justin uses for booking). The agent reads the now-open slot, checks the waitlist of students who've requested that teacher or instrument, factors in lesson type (30-min vs 60-min, makeup vs new), and sends a batched SMS to the top three candidates with a one-tap booking link. Stack is the Opus1 webhook (or polling if no webhook) plus n8n plus Twilio plus Claude for the ranking logic plus a small Next.js booking page. Around $150-250 per month. Replaces Justin or his front-desk person manually texting the waitlist after each cancellation, which usually means it doesn't happen and the slot goes empty. **Pitch:** every cancelled slot gets re-filled in under 15 minutes from the waitlist that's already on your phone, without you texting anyone.

**SMB translation — Jenn (IEC):** Counseling slots are her revenue. When a senior cancels their Wednesday session, the trigger fires, the agent scans the waitlist of juniors and sophomores who flagged "want more time on essays" or "need to talk through college list," ranks by deadline proximity (someone with a Nov 1 deadline outranks someone with a Jan 15 deadline), and sends a one-tap booking link. Stack is whatever calendar Jenn uses (Calendly, Acuity, or a custom layer on Squarespace) plus n8n plus Twilio plus Claude for the deadline-aware ranking. Around $100-200 per month. Replaces nothing — most IECs don't run a waitlist at all, the slot just goes empty. **Pitch:** the slot a student cancels gets sold to a student with a closer deadline before lunch.

**SMB translation — Caped (Debbie, SAT/ACT prep):** Tutoring is even more cancellation-prone than counseling because students cram around test dates. When a tutor's 5pm slot opens, the agent ranks waitlist students by the proximity of their test date and any score gap they've reported, then offers the slot to the student whose June 7 test is closest with the biggest math gap to close. Stack is the tutoring scheduling tool plus webhook plus Claude plus SMS. Around $200-400 per month for a small tutoring shop. Replaces Debbie or the office manager manually calling around to fill cancellations. **Pitch:** the kid taking the SAT next Saturday gets the slot the kid taking it in October dropped, automatically.
