# 2026-10-13 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
Cooley and Google Cloud announced a pre-filing redaction agent on Oct 8 that proposes targeted redactions of PII and privileged content for attorney review before a court submission — a clean example of an agent as a gatekeeper step in a workflow that is otherwise all human eyeballs. Starling Bank's Scam Intelligence agent is the other standout: it injects a Socratic conversation into the middle of an outgoing payment, not a warning banner, and extended an earlier marketplace-listing version that tripled payment-cancellation rates.

## Patterns

### 1. Conversational friction injected mid-payment to interrupt scam transfers
**Source:** [Starling Bank press release, Jun 24 2026](https://www.starlingbank.com/news/new-ai-feature-detects-romance-scammers/) — Scam Intelligence rolled out inside the Starling Assistant to all five million UK customers; detects ten scam types including romance, pension, investment, and deepfake. Earlier 2025 version that scanned marketplace listings reportedly raised customer-initiated cancellation of suspicious marketplace payments by about 300%.
**Mechanism:** Customer describes an outgoing payment or counterparty in natural language to the in-app assistant → agent recognizes transfer intent and the scam archetype in play → asks pointed follow-up questions tailored to that archetype (for a romance scenario it asks why the partner can't pay, where you met, how long you've known them) → scores likelihood of fraud and recommends a pause plus a handoff to the bank's fraud team → writes the interaction and risk flag into the customer record and fraud queue. Replaces the bank's reactive post-chargeback investigation and the generic "are you sure?" confirmation screens.
**Why agentic:** The agent reasons about which scam pattern applies, generates a case-specific line of questioning, and chooses when to escalate; it is not a static rule or a dashboard surfacing a risk score. The transfer flow is actually interrupted and rerouted based on the conversation.
**Generalizable core:** Transaction intent expressed in natural language → agent classifies the risk archetype → runs an archetype-specific interrogation script → blocks or reroutes the transaction and writes evidence to the case system → replaces post-event investigation and one-size-fits-all confirmations.
**Deployability:** Needs the trigger to live inside a conversational entry point to the transaction (chat assistant, voice IVR, or an in-app assistant that fronts the "pay" button). Build on Claude or Gemini with a small scam-archetype router plus archetype-specific prompt templates and an escalation webhook into the transaction system. Weekend prototype on Zapier/n8n for the archetype routing + payment-system hold; week to month build to get the interrogation prompts tight and to wire into a real transaction gate. Augments or offsets fraud ops headcount; does not replace the fraud team but changes where their time lands.

### 2. Pre-submission redaction gate for court filings
**Source:** [Cooley / Google Cloud announcement, Oct 8 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/coverage/2026/2026-10-08-cooley-collaborates-with-google-cloud-to-develop-ai-agent-for-complex-litigation) — Gemini Enterprise for Legal agent in co-development with Cooley litigators; attorneys make all final redaction calls. Error rate and GA date not published; [ComplexDiscovery coverage](https://complexdiscovery.com/cooley-and-google-cloud-are-building-a-court-filing-redaction-agent-without-a-published-error-rate/) flags that gap.
**Mechanism:** Associate uploads a filing draft and the connected exhibits/discovery material → agent scans for personally identifiable information, confidential technical details, and potentially privileged content → proposes targeted redactions in context (not blanket blackouts) with a rationale tied to each span → writes a marked-up draft back to the matter folder for the attorney to accept, reject, or edit → attorney submits the human-approved version to the court. Replaces the junior-associate pass that currently eats days per filing.
**Why agentic:** The redaction choices are classification + contextual judgment calls across a long document, not a regex pass. Each proposed span is tied to a category and reviewable, and the agent operates as a required step in the filing workflow rather than a tool a lawyer opens on demand.
**Generalizable core:** Document about to leave the firm's trust boundary → agent reads full content plus any linked sources → proposes category-tagged redactions or masks for human sign-off → writes a reviewable draft back to the system of record → replaces a slow, expensive manual review that gates external release.
**Deployability:** The pattern generalizes to any outbound-document chokepoint: contract exhibits before counter-party review, medical records before transfer, procurement RFPs before vendor send, PR drafts before publication. A week-build on Claude or Gemini with structured outputs (span offsets + category labels) and a reviewer UI; a month to month-plus to hit domain-grade recall and to earn trust for the gatekeeper position. Augments associates and paralegals, compliance officers, or whoever currently owns the pre-send scrub; a human in the loop stays by design, especially for regulated outputs.

### 3. Vision agent that grades physical goods at a receiving dock
**Source:** [Albertsons / Google Cloud announcement, May 13 2026](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-05-13-Albertsons-Companies-Announces-AI-Powered-Supply-Chain-Tool-to-Further-Enhance-Produce-Quality-Control-and-Consistency); [The Packer coverage](https://www.thepacker.com/news/retail/albertsons-debuts-ai-tool-boost-produce-quality) — Intelligent Quality Control tool is live in select DCs for strawberries and red/green grapes, built on Gemini Enterprise Vision AI. Albertsons operates 22 DCs serving 2,244 stores; expansion planned across the berry category then nationwide.
**Mechanism:** Pallet arrives at the inspection station → inspector takes a photo on a handheld and uploads it → vision model compares against Albertsons' internal visual quality standards for that SKU (bruising, mold, color, size uniformity) → agent returns a quality rating plus an accept/reject/regrade recommendation → inspector approves and the decision writes back to the DC quality-control system of record. Replaces per-inspector subjective judgment that drifts across people, shifts, and sites.
**Why agentic:** The agent carries codified quality standards and makes a reasoned grading decision per pallet, not a passive dashboard of defect photos. The decision has consequences (accept/reject/regrade) and writes into the operational system. The inspector remains the final approver, keeping the human-in-the-loop pattern.
**Generalizable core:** Physical arrival or completion event → operator photographs the thing → vision+LLM agent compares against a codified visual standard → writes a grade or accept/reject decision into the operational system of record → replaces the inconsistency of individual subjective inspection.
**Deployability:** The expensive piece is the codified visual standard per SKU; the agent plumbing is routine. Buildable with Claude multimodal or Gemini plus a small reviewer app for a weekend prototype on one SKU; expanding across SKU families and training the standard is a month-plus. Augments and standardizes QC inspectors; probably doesn't eliminate them but changes consistency and training burden. Scale caveat: multi-site operation makes the standardization gain much bigger — a single-location shop sees less lift because the per-inspector variance is lower.

### 4. Deterministic agent chain that drafts long analytical documents
**Source:** [DBS Bank, Aug 2026 via The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/press-releases/dbs-rolls-out-agentic-ai-tool-to-support-corporate-credit-assessment-process) — rolled out from a 150-user pilot to about 1,500 bankers and credit risk managers globally. DBS estimates credit memos consume up to 40% of relationship manager time; targeting at least a 30% reduction.
**Mechanism:** Relationship manager initiates a corporate credit review for a specific client → an orchestrator kicks off 70+ specialized agents in a deterministic chain, each doing one narrow job (pull annual reports, extract ratios, summarize industry research, surface internal relationship history, flag covenant risks, draft each memo section) → outputs are stitched into a review-ready first-draft memo → draft lands in the RM's queue for human refinement with industry judgment and client context → final memo flows into the credit decision system. Replaces most of the research-and-assemble labor; keeps the judgment work human.
**Why agentic:** Each agent takes an action against external and internal data sources, not a document display or a chat completion; the orchestration itself is the product, and the chain is explicit and auditable rather than one big LLM "do it all" call. It's the opposite architectural choice from autonomous reasoning agents, and it's working at scale.
**Generalizable core:** Human kicks off a long, structured analytical document → orchestrator fires an explicit chain of narrow task-agents against known sources → each writes into pre-defined document sections → human owns judgment, context, and final sign-off → replaces the research and assembly phase of a knowledge-worker deliverable.
**Deployability:** Deterministic chaining is a design philosophy you can run on any orchestrator (n8n, custom Python, Claude Agent SDK, LangGraph). Weekend-buildable for a two-to-five-agent chain producing a modest deliverable (a one-page vendor diligence memo, an inspection report, a weekly client digest); grows to a month-plus to hit 70+ agents with proper handoffs, state, and governance. Pattern applies anywhere a human currently assembles a long document from mostly-known sources: insurance underwriting memos, M&A diligence summaries, grant applications, inspection reports, investor update letters. Augments the senior analyst or RM; the time saved goes to judgment work, which is why DBS reports the top-of-funnel number (40% of RM time) rather than headcount.
