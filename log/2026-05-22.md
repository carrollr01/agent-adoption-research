# 2026-05-22 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
Two of today's three patterns map directly to Sollos: a CPG back-office agent workforce that logs into Shopify, Amazon Seller Central, and the brand's ERP to run order intake, demand planning, and finance reconciliation; and a deduction-recovery agent that navigates retailer portals to dispute invalid chargebacks. The third — Fieldproxy's multi-week recurring-route optimizer for pest control and landscaping — reshuffles the entire service calendar when one quarterly customer reschedules, not just today's stops.

## Patterns

### 1. The CPG back-office agent workforce that runs the operations stack — `sollos`

**Source:** [Corvera launch (YC W26)](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/PES-corvera-ai-agents-for-cpg-brands) + [TechCrunch coverage of Glimpse $35M Series A](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/a16z-backed-glimpse-raises-new-funds-accelerates-dispute-tracking-automation-for-cpg-brands/) — Corvera went $0 → $33k MRR in 4 weeks across 12 brands; Glimpse now serves 200+ CPG brands.

**Mechanism:** A purchase order arrives by email or EDI. The agent parses it, checks line items against the brand's pricing sheet and current inventory in NetSuite or Cin7, enters the order into the ERP, sends the pick-pack instructions to the 3PL via their portal or API, and writes the tracking number back to Shopify/Amazon Seller Central. In parallel, a separate agent pulls daily sales from Amazon, Shopify, and retailer EDI feeds, recomputes the rolling 90-day forecast with seasonal and promo factors, and writes the updated forecast to the same ERP. A third agent reconciles incoming settlements (Amazon, Faire, Shopify Payments) against open invoices and flags margin variances by SKU and channel. The agents log in like a human employee — username, password, MFA — and follow the brand's SOP rather than requiring a custom integration. Replaces the ops manager doing daily order entry, the planner running spreadsheet forecasts, and the bookkeeper reconciling channel settlements.

**Why agentic:** Each task has a real decision point. Order parsing has to handle non-standard PO formats and missing SKUs (route to human or substitute?). Forecasting reweights live signals on a continuous loop. Reconciliation requires three-way matching across channel reports, invoices, and bank deposits, with judgment about which mismatches are real. A dashboard would surface the discrepancies; the agent resolves them.

**SMB translation:** Sollos is pre-launch yerba mate, so the immediate version is narrower: one agent that watches the Shopify orders feed plus the Sollos Gmail inbox, parses wholesale POs from cafes and convenience stores, enters them into a Cin7 or Airtable inventory record, and emails the 3PL/co-packer with the day's pick list. Stack is n8n or Make polling Gmail and Shopify, Claude API parsing PO emails into structured JSON, Cin7 or Airtable as the inventory system of record, and a Slack channel for any line items the agent isn't confident about. Realistic cost is $300-500/month at Sollos's volume, scaling to a Corvera-style $1-3k/month once they're on shelves with Whole Foods or Sprouts and EDI compliance becomes mandatory. Replaces the "Stephen-does-everything" role across order intake and inventory updates. Pitch sentence: every order from the Shopify popup, the cafe wholesale form, and the inbox lands in your inventory system before you've finished your morning yerba — and when you start shipping into retail, the same agent stack handles the EDI 850s without you hiring an ops person.

### 2. The retailer-portal deduction agent that disputes invalid chargebacks autonomously — `sollos` / `general`

**Source:** [Glimpse platform overview](https://www.tryglimpse.com/) — one agent reviewed 17,000 deductions in under 24 hours for a $1B CPG, recovered $10M+; [RetailPath](https://retailpath.xyz/) reports ~86% dispute win rate. Glimpse pivoted from an earlier YC product and just closed $35M from a16z.

**Mechanism:** Trigger is a new deduction line on a remittance from Walmart, Target, KeHE, UNFI, etc. — pulled either from the brand's bank reconciliation (a payment came in $X short) or scraped directly from the retailer portal (Retail Link, Partners Online, Walgreens Direct). The agent logs into the portal, downloads the deduction backup (claim code, invoice number, BOL, POD), classifies the deduction type (shortage, OTIF fine, pricing claim, MCB), and runs three-way matching: retailer's claim vs. brand's original invoice vs. shipping documentation from the 3PL/EDI 856. If the math says the deduction is invalid (wrong claim code, valid POD with full quantity, EDI mismatch), the agent assembles a dispute packet with the supporting documents and submits it through the same portal. It writes the claim status back to QuickBooks or NetSuite and tracks the dispute through resolution. Replaces the AR specialist who manually downloads PDFs from Retail Link, the deduction analyst who classifies and routes, and the trade marketing coordinator who chases credit memos.

**Why agentic:** Retailer portals don't have APIs. The work requires a browser session, captcha solving, classifying free-text claim codes into known invalid/valid categories, and reasoning across three documents to build a dispute narrative. Reference data from Retail Value Chain Federation: 65-80% of retail shortage claims are invalid — without an agent, the time cost of disputing exceeds the recovery on each individual claim and the brand writes them off.

**SMB translation:** Doesn't apply to Sollos today (no retailers, no chargebacks), but maps directly to their first big distribution win. Tag it for the deck and bring it up when Sollos signs their first KeHE or UNFI account — that's the moment deductions become real and a one-person CPG team gets buried. Realistic stack at a 5-25 person CPG: Playwright or Browserbase session on retailer portals, Claude with Sonnet 4.6 doing claim classification and dispute drafting, Airtable or Postgres tracking dispute pipeline, QuickBooks integration for cash application. Around $400-1,200/month for brands with $2M-15M in revenue across 2-4 retailers, vs. Glimpse's enterprise pricing aimed at $50M+ brands. Replaces the part-time AR/deduction analyst the brand can't afford to hire. Pitch sentence: 65-80% of the deductions on your KeHE remittance are wrong, you're writing them off because chasing them costs more than they're worth — we make the agent that disputes them on autopilot so your margin stays where you priced it.

### 3. The multi-week recurring-route optimizer that reshuffles the calendar when one customer moves — `pest-control` / `landscaping` / `hvac`

**Source:** [Fieldproxy 2026 pest control software roundup](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/resources/blog/best-pest-control-software-ai-routing-2026) — companies switching report 18-25% improvements in route density vs. their previous manual/semi-automated routing.

**Mechanism:** Recurring-service businesses have a customer base where each property is on a cadence — monthly pest control, quarterly perimeter, weekly lawn, biannual HVAC tune-up. The trigger is a calendar mutation: a customer reschedules, a tech calls out, a new contract starts, or a quarterly service is overdue. A traditional routing engine optimizes today's stops in isolation. The agent considers the full multi-week calendar simultaneously — when a quarterly customer moves their May appointment to June, it recalculates the routing impact for that quarterly customer's August slot, the monthly customers two blocks over whose route density was anchored to that visit, and the weekly customers downstream. It reads from ServiceTitan, FieldRoutes, Jobber, or Housecall Pro for the customer list, service cadence, and tech availability. It writes back the new appointment slots, generates the SMS/email confirmations to affected customers, and reissues the tech's route sheets. Replaces the dispatcher manually playing whack-a-mole with reschedule requests and the operations manager rebuilding the master schedule every Sunday.

**Why agentic:** The optimization isn't just a one-time TSP solve. It runs continuously against a live calendar, has to decide which customers to nudge to a different slot (price-sensitive ones, ones whose contracts allow flexibility), and has to know when a reschedule would push a quarterly customer past their contract window. The output isn't a chart — it's an updated set of appointments, customer notifications, and tech routes.

**SMB translation:** Direct fit for the cold-outreach landscaping and pest-control verticals. Realistic trigger at a 5-30 truck operation: any change in Jobber or Housecall Pro (customer reschedule request, tech callout) fires a webhook to n8n, which spins up a Claude-driven loop with access to the customer database, the tech roster, the geocoded service map (Mapbox or Google Maps Distance Matrix API), and the cadence rules per contract. Agent proposes a new multi-week plan, surfaces a one-screen diff to the dispatcher for approval, and on approval pushes updates back to Jobber and the customer SMS channel. Around $500-900/month for 5-15 trucks, $1.2-2k/month for 15-50 trucks. Replaces the full-time dispatcher at smaller shops and frees the operations manager at larger ones. Pitch sentence: when your Tuesday customer reschedules to Thursday, your agent rebuilds the next four weeks of routes for every customer their cadence touches — your trucks get one more stop a day and your dispatcher stops working Sundays.
