Five patterns worth logging today. The strongest signal came from a YC S26 restaurant back-of-house company (Truffle) that closes the loop from computer-vision inventory counts to autonomous purchase orders and invoice reconciliation. The most mechanically interesting is Mozilla's Firefox bug-hunting harness on How I AI: the trick isn't the model, it's forcing the agent to write and run a reproducing test case before a finding counts. Engineers credit half the win to the harness, not the model. That reproducibility gate is a copy-paste pattern for any review task where a verification step is executable — accounting anomalies re-run the trial balance, legal reviewers must quote the section they inspected.

Attio's June Workflows relaunch is the third pattern worth thinking about: an operator types a sentence, an agent compiles it into a workflow graph, and a separate agent runs it against the full customer context every time it fires. The workflow itself is generated, not designed. For SMBs on HubSpot or Pipedrive, this maps to Claude + MCP servers over their SaaS — the internal ops person who spent Wednesday afternoons wiring Zaps gets replaced by a text box.

Two more worth having in the toolkit: Latchel's maintenance triage in property management runs a category-specific troubleshooting script with the tenant before dispatching a truck, closing 23% of tickets at intake and de-escalating 48% of "emergencies." The generalizable pattern is "agent attempts the resolution before scheduling the action," and it copies straight into dental symptom intake, vet triage, and tier-1 IT support. FlowManual (YC S26) reads HVAC and MEP contract packages — bids, specs, invoices — extracts a structured takeoff, and writes back clarifying questions to the general contractor before an estimator opens the file. Same shape works for insurance submissions, legal contract intake, and tax K-1 packages.
