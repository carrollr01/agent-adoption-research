# 2026-04-26 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
The most useful pattern surfacing in late April is methodological: Vercel and SaaStr both built outbound sales agents by shadowing their best human for six weeks and documenting every step before anyone wrote a line of agent code. The same recipe drops into Jenn's IEC practice and Capital Educators — when the operator is the playbook, document the playbook. Two more patterns are concrete enough to use in discovery: Notion 3.4 Custom Agent Autofill (databases that maintain themselves), and the dormant-lead reactivation agent SaaStr is running at 70% response rates.

## Patterns

### 1. The agent that mirrors your top performer's playbook — `jenn`, `caped`, `music-space`

**Source:** [Vercel: What we learned building agents](https://vercel.com/blog/what-we-learned-building-agents) and [Lenny's: SaaStr's 20 AI agents](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/we-replaced-our-sales-team-with-20-ai-agents) — Vercel cut a 10-person SDR team to 1; SaaStr's agents now do work formerly handled by 10 SDRs and AEs supervised by 1.2 humans.

**Mechanism:** Six weeks of shadowing one top performer, documenting every step they took on every inbound (the questions they asked, the data they pulled, the order they did things in, the language they used in replies). That documentation becomes the agent's operating procedure. At runtime, an inbound message triggers the agent to filter spam, query internal databases for context, run Deep Research on the company or person, draft a personalized response in the documented voice, and route into Slack for a human supervisor to approve and send. The agent learns from supervisor edits over time. Vercel reports the agent now drafts most outbound; SaaStr reports 70K hyper-personalized emails per period vs. 7K from humans, and AI-generated emails outperforming human-written ones.

**Why agentic:** The trigger is the inbound (not a button), the agent reads multiple data sources, takes a sequenced action (filter → enrich → draft → route), and writes back into Slack and CRM. The dashboard equivalent would be a CRM view showing inbound leads — useless without the action chain.

**SMB translation:** Jenn is the top performer in her IEC practice. The setup is to spend three to four weeks recording her process for the routine 70% of student work — the activity-list review, the school-list narrowing pass, the essay-prompt brainstorm reply, the parent-update email. Either video screen-recordings she narrates, or annotated transcripts of how she handles five real student threads. That corpus becomes the agent's playbook. At runtime, the agent reads new student emails or Slack messages, pulls the student's record from her tracker, and drafts a reply in her voice as a Gmail draft labeled `agent-draft`. She edits and sends — same as Vercel's Slack approval loop. Stack is Gmail + Google Drive playbook + Claude Sonnet + a thin Notion or Airtable shim for the student tracker. Around $80–150/month at her volume. Replaces about 60–90 minutes a day of "writing the same email I wrote last Tuesday." Pitch sentence: clone your voice for the 70% of routine student work so you spend the other 30% on the strategic calls only you can have.

Same setup at Capital Educators with Debbie — document how she handles diagnostic test debriefs, score-plateau parent calls, and the August 1 "we still have slots" reactivation cycle. Around $150–300/month at her volume. Replaces a junior tutor's first three months of ramp.

At The Music Space with Justin — the playbook is how he handles trial-lesson follow-ups, parent recital questions, and tuition-bump conversations. The recordings are what he already says on the phone. Around $80–120/month. Replaces the 9 PM "I'll get back to all those parents tomorrow" he never quite gets to.

### 2. The Notion database that maintains itself — `jenn`, `ls-custom`, `caped`, `music-space`

**Source:** [Notion 3.4 release notes, April 14, 2026](https://www.notion.com/releases/2026-04-14) and [Notion AI Autofill help](https://www.notion.com/help/autofill) — Custom Agent Autofill went out April 14, runs autonomously on schedule or row-trigger, and is 35–50% cheaper than the prior implementation. Free to try until May 3, then folds into Notion Business credits.

**Mechanism:** The trigger is either a database row being created or modified, or a scheduled cron the agent owner sets. The agent reads the row plus any linked pages or external sources connected via MCP (Slack threads, Gmail, Calendar, Linear), runs a multi-step prompt the operator has saved as a property fill, and writes the result back into a column on that row. The Notion team's framing is that the database "stays trustworthy without manual review." Concretely: a Sales Lead row gets an enriched company description, a Last Contact summary, and a Next Action recommendation written automatically every morning, derived from the email thread and the CRM record.

**Why agentic:** It's not a chat interface. It's a property fill that runs without a human prompt, on schedule, against live data sources, and writes structured output into operational records. The dashboard equivalent would be a Notion view that surfaces stale rows and asks the operator to update them.

**SMB translation:** Jenn's student tracker today is a Google Sheet she updates Sunday nights. Move it to a Notion database with one row per student. Set Custom Agent Autofill on three columns: `current_essay_status` (reads the most recent Gmail thread with the student and their parents, summarizes where they are in the essay cycle), `next_deadline_30d` (reads the school-list page and surfaces what's actually due in the next thirty days for that student), and `flags_to_review` (anything ambiguous or off-pace). Stack is Notion Business plus Custom Agent runtime, around $30/seat for Notion plus roughly $58/month per agent in runtime once the free trial ends, plus token costs. Replaces the Sunday spreadsheet update session and the "wait, where is Sophie on her Common App?" recall problem on Monday morning calls. Pitch sentence: every student row tells you the truth Monday morning without you opening Gmail.

Same setup at LS Custom or any IEC practice tracking 30+ students at a time. Same at Capital Educators where the rows are SAT/ACT students and the autofill columns are `last_practice_score`, `score_trend_4wk`, and `next_recommended_test_date` — all readable from the Bluebook score export plus the tutor session notes.

At The Music Space — rows are students, autofill columns are `weeks_since_last_lesson_change`, `parent_engagement_signal_30d` (last parent email tone), and `recital_readiness_flag`. Replaces the studio manager's monthly "who's at risk of churning" review. Around $50/month total.

### 3. The reactivation agent that wakes up your dead leads — `jenn`, `caped`, `music-space`, `sollos`

**Source:** [Lenny's Podcast: SaaStr replaced its sales team with 20 AI agents](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/we-replaced-our-sales-team-with-20-ai-agents) — Jason Lemkin's most cited result is that the agents reactivate leads humans had written off, hitting 70% response rates on cohorts the SDRs had marked as not worth their time.

**Mechanism:** Trigger is a scheduled scan of the CRM for leads aged past a threshold (60, 90, 180 days) without contact. For each, the agent reads the original conversation thread, any product or service updates since, and the lead's company or context (LinkedIn, public news), then drafts a hyper-personalized reactivation email referencing the specific thing they cared about and a specific reason to come back now. Sends through the operator's Gmail, tracks opens and replies, escalates positive replies to a human in Slack, archives the rest. SaaStr reports 70K of these emails sent at the volume one human could do 7K of.

**Why agentic:** It's outbound on a schedule with per-lead reasoning, not a templated drip. Each email is grounded in that lead's specific history. The dashboard equivalent would be a CRM segment list of stale leads, which is what every CRM already has and what nobody acts on.

**SMB translation:** Jenn — every fall, there's a backlog of inquiries from spring and summer that didn't convert. Parents who got a quote, said "we'll think about it," and never came back. The agent runs every Sunday night, reads the original email thread, checks whether the family has any signal of where their kid landed (an Instagram post, a school news item, nothing — that's fine too), and drafts a one-line check-in: "saw Tess is a junior now — we still have a few August-start slots if you'd like to pick the conversation back up." Stack is whatever CRM Jenn has (HubSpot Free or Pipedrive Lite) plus a small Claude script plus Gmail send. Around $50–80/month. Replaces the Sunday-night guilt of "should I email those people from May." Pitch sentence: every lead that ghosted you in spring gets one well-timed touch in the fall while you sleep.

Same at Capital Educators — Debbie's stale leads are families who took a diagnostic and never enrolled. Reactivation agent runs in late July when score panic peaks, drafts personalized notes referencing the specific test the kid took and their score range. Around $80–120/month.

At The Music Space — stale leads are families who came in for a trial lesson but didn't sign up. Trigger seasonally (back-to-school in August, post-holidays in January). Around $50/month.

At Sollos — the reactivation list is buyers Stephen pitched at trade shows or sampled to who didn't convert. Trigger is when there's a real reason to come back: new flavor, new packaging, new retail win. Agent drafts a personalized note referencing what each buyer specifically cared about in the original conversation. Around $80–120/month. Replaces the spreadsheet of "people I should follow up with" Stephen looks at and feels bad about.
