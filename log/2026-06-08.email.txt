Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-06-08 — 3 patterns | fully autonomous HVAC dispatch at Gulfshore Air

https://github.com/a3-dev/agent-adoption-research/blob/main/log/2026-06-08.md

Three concrete patterns today. The headline: ServiceTitan and Gulfshore Air Conditioning ran the first call-to-payment HVAC job with zero office staff touching it. Qualia shipped Clear 2.0 with specialized title agents that actually pick up the phone to collect mortgage payoffs and HOA estoppels. And the bank reconciliation agent went production-ready across Lunos, HighRadius, and Microsoft Dynamics 365 with the same shape — auto-post the timing differences, only escalate real breaks.

Pattern 1 — the autonomous dispatch chain. Gulfshore is a 30-person Florida contractor. An inbound call hit ServiceTitan's voice agent FI, which diagnosed a 10-year-old AC and handed off to Phin, which checked equipment history and real-time tech capacity, booked a same-day slot, dispatched a tech whose bio reached the homeowner before arrival, turned on Sales Pro when the truck pinged the address, captured $1,200 through ServiceTitan Payments, and fed the recording back into post-job analytics. The new insight isn't the voice receptionist — that's old. It's the unbroken handoff chain where each system surfaces just enough context for the next agent to act. For a plumbing or electrical contractor on Housecall Pro or Jobber instead of ServiceTitan, the same shape runs $300–600/month with a Vapi or Retell front door, dispatch logic inside the FSM, Twilio SMS confirms, and Stripe Terminal at the truck. Pitch: your phones run themselves at lunch and after 5pm — same-day jobs book, dispatch, and pay out before anyone in the office sees a calendar invite. Same shape works for pest control (route density triggered the night before by recurring contracts plus seasonal pest pressure plus geography), mobile vets, locksmiths, and landscaping (weather triggers a route reshuffle).

Pattern 2 — specialized closing agents that pick up the phone for you. Qualia Clear 2.0 has four specialists: a property tax agent that scans county websites, an entity verification agent for LLC good standing, and a mortgage payoff agent plus HOA estoppel agent that make outbound phone calls through marketplace vendors to collect payoffs and estoppel letters. Housingwire's writeup puts time savings at 35–50% per file. The translatable insight is "an agent that calls a third party on your behalf to extract a single structured field." For a small mortgage broker or insurance agency, the same shape pulls payoffs, loss runs, and binder confirmations — trigger is a new file in the LOS or AMS, action is a Vapi or Retell call with a tight script and structured extraction, output is a field written back to the system of record. Stack is Vapi/Retell plus Claude for extraction plus Make or n8n for the writeback, $200–500/month per field type. Replaces the processor whose week is mostly hold music. Same shape for vet clinics chasing referring-vet records and pediatric specialists chasing insurance pre-auths.

Pattern 3 — the reconciliation agent that only escalates real breaks. Lunos AI, HighRadius, and Microsoft's Account Reconciliation Agent in Dynamics 365 all shipped the same mechanism in the last few weeks: continuously ingest bank feeds, compare against the GL, separate timing differences from real breaks, auto-post adjusting entries to the ERP for timing items, route unmatched items to an accountant by type or dollar threshold. Lunos claims 75% reduction in manual AR workload; Beancount cites teams collapsing 12-day closes to 3 days. For a small CPA firm running QuickBooks Online across 30–60 clients, $400–800/month gets you the matching layer plus a Slack escalation queue with a configurable dollar line for partner-level review. Replaces the bookkeeper chasing $14 Stripe-fee variances for two days a month. Pitch: your team only touches reconciliation items that actually need judgment — everything else is posted and audited before your morning coffee. Restaurants running Toast or Square against a QuickBooks ledger catch chargebacks inside the dispute window. Multi-location retailers stop missing deposit-mismatch alarms. Small nonprofits get a clean 990 instead of a panicky September.

Sources of note: ServiceTitan's Gulfshore Air blog post and the Qualia Clear 2.0 launch are the strongest operator-level signals this week. The reconciliation pattern is convergent — three platforms shipped within a few weeks of each other, which usually means the SMB-ready version is six months out.
