# 2026-05-20 — Agent Pattern Scan

## TL;DR
The counselor question-log agent (Eva from College Guidance Network) answers student/family questions 24/7 and produces a per-student question log that the counselor walks into the next session already holding. Direct fit for Jenn — closest competitor pattern we've seen yet for the IEC vertical.

## Patterns

### 1. The counselor question-log agent that briefs you before the next session — jenn
Source: https://www.usnews.com/education/u-s-news-higher-ground/articles/2026-05-14/ai-admissions-college-counselor — College Guidance Network's Eva, deployed across a counselor network spanning 300+ practitioners and several school districts as of mid-2026.
Mechanism: Student or parent asks Eva a question (chat, any hour) → Eva reads the student profile (grade, target schools, prior conversations) plus the CGN knowledge base → answers the question and, in the same loop, updates a roadmap document attached to that student → appends the question and Eva's answer to a counselor-facing question log → on the morning of the next 1:1, the counselor opens the log and sees what the family has been stuck on for the past two weeks. Replaces the "what's the FAFSA deadline / what's a Why-Northwestern essay" inbox flood that eats Jenn's evenings, and replaces the cold start of every session.
Why agentic: Two-sided output (student-facing answer + counselor-facing artifact) and the loop persists state across conversations. Not a chatbot, not a dashboard — the counselor's pre-session brief is generated as a side effect of student conversations.
SMB translation: Jenn / IECs. Trigger is a parent text or email to the practice's shared inbox (Jenn already uses email + a Squarespace inquiry form). Stack: Claude API + a thin Next.js front-end embedded on the practice site, plus a per-student doc in Google Drive that the agent appends to. Question log delivered as a one-page PDF dropped into the counselor's calendar event for that student. Replaces the unpaid "quick question" labor that Jenn currently absorbs between sessions, plus the 10 minutes of "where did we leave off" at the start of every meeting. Pitch sentence: "Every session starts with you already knowing what the family has been Googling for the last two weeks, and the family stops emailing you at 10pm because they can ask you-but-not-you instead."

### 2. The in-home tech revenue agent that briefs, listens, and writes up the visit — hvac, landscaping, pest-control
Source: https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/robby — Robby, YC W26. Paying home-services customers, claiming six-figure additional revenue per technician per week from upsell opportunities surfaced during normal visits.
Mechanism: Job is dispatched in ServiceTitan or comparable FSM → agent pulls a pre-job briefing for the tech (equipment age at the address, prior tickets, neighborhood patterns) → during the visit the tech taps a button on the phone and the agent listens to the room, watching for upsell-relevant cues (aging boiler next to the serviced furnace, mold smell, dated panel) → after the visit the agent writes the job documentation, logs identified opportunities back to the FSM, and queues a follow-up estimate. Replaces the dispatcher's manual pre-job research, the manager's upsell coaching, and the tech's evening paperwork.
Why agentic: Runs across three phases (pre-visit research, during-visit listening, post-visit writeup) with autonomous decisions at each — what's worth briefing on, what counts as an upsell signal, what writeup format the FSM expects. Writes to a system of record, not a chat window.
SMB translation: Cold-outreach verticals — landscaping, HVAC, pest control. At a 5-tech landscaping shop the trigger is "job marked en route in Jobber." Stack is Jobber webhook → Claude API for the pre-job brief + a Twilio voice channel for the during-visit listening + a writeback into Jobber via their API + n8n to glue it. Budget: $400-800/month including LLM costs. Replaces the office manager's morning route briefings and the owner's Sunday-night writeup catchup. Pitch sentence: "Your techs walk into every house already knowing the equipment's age, the agent flags the upsell while they're standing in the basement, and Sunday-night paperwork stops happening because the writeup is done by the time they're back in the truck."

### 3. The field-notes-to-estimate agent that turns a voice memo into a priced quote — landscaping, pest-control, hvac
Source: https://ravensightai.com/how-ai-can-help-landscaping-estimate-creation-automation/ and https://myquoteiq.com/ai-estimator/ — two vendor implementations targeting small landscape contractors and adjacent trades. QuoteIQ uses customer-uploaded photos + zip-coded market pricing; Ravensight uses crew-recorded voice memos plus the contractor's own price book.
Mechanism: Crew records a 20-second voice memo from the truck and snaps 3-5 property photos → agent transcribes, extracts work-type, quantities, materials, access constraints → matches line items against the contractor's loaded price book (or, for QuoteIQ, against zip-level market pricing) → drafts an itemized PDF estimate, suggests pair-with upsells (gutters with a lawn quote, driveway treatment with a sealcoat) → routes to the owner for approval or sends directly to the homeowner. Writes the opportunity into the CRM. Replaces the 30-90 minute end-of-day session where the owner re-keys crew notes into QuickBooks and writes the quote.
Why agentic: Voice/photo ingest is non-deterministic; the system reasons about which line items apply, which upsells make sense for this property, and whether to send or hold for review. Not a form-filler.
SMB translation: Landscaping primary (Ravensight is $199/month entry). Trigger is the crew tapping a "new estimate" shortcut on their phone. Stack: Twilio for the voice memo intake + Claude API with a structured-output schema for line items + the contractor's existing QuickBooks/Jobber catalog as the price source + a PDF generator (the same kind of small Next.js renderer we used for Sollos label work). Budget: $300-600/month all-in. Replaces the owner's "office time" (every landscaper we've talked to hates this hour) and the spreadsheet-driven quoting workflow most one-truck shops use today. Pitch sentence: "Your crew talks into the phone walking back to the truck, the homeowner has a priced quote in their inbox before the next stop, and you never spend Sunday catching up on estimates again." Note for the cold outreach calendar: same scaffold transfers to pest control (read inspection photos → priced recurring-service quote) with no architectural change.

### 4. The course-page-to-student-agent-suite generator — caped, jenn
Source: https://www.producthunt.com/products/coursekit and https://coursekit.productizeyourmind.com/ — recent Product Hunt launch; mechanism is concrete though we have not confirmed a named paying customer at scale.
Mechanism: Course creator pastes their sales-page URL (Teachable, Thinkific, Kajabi, Circle, Podia, Skool) → agent crawls the page and extracts modules, outcomes, branded methodology, the creator's "framework" language, brand colors → auto-generates a suite of student-facing agents that mirror the course's framework (e.g., "Apply the 4-step method to your situation," "Get unstuck on Module 3"), each as an embeddable widget → creator reviews tool names/descriptions, toggles each on, drops the embed into the course platform. Replaces 1:1 student check-ins on framework application and the prompt-engineering work most course creators won't do themselves.
Why agentic: Two layers — a meta-agent that generates the tool suite from the sales page, and a set of persistent student-facing agents that then run inside the course 24/7. The meta layer reasons about which student-facing tools to spawn given the course content.
SMB translation: Capital Educators (SAT/ACT prep) and IECs who sell digital courses. For Capital, the trigger is "Debbie launches a new section of her course on the digital SAT." Stack: a single Claude API endpoint that reads the syllabus + sample lessons and writes a JSON config of N student-facing agents, each backed by a system prompt with the course's framework baked in; widgets embedded as iframes inside the existing course platform. Budget: $400-700/month for a multi-course operator, dominated by per-student LLM usage. Replaces the time prep instructors spend answering the same conceptual questions across cohorts (why this answer choice is wrong, how to set up this kind of problem) and the assistant who currently runs office hours. Pitch sentence: "Every student gets a coach that knows your method by name, you stop answering the same five questions across cohorts, and your course feels alive even when you're not in front of it."
