Agent Pattern Scan — 2026-05-05 — 4 patterns | "in-good-order" intake agent for insurance/mortgage/medical billing

Four patterns today. The freshest is the "in-good-order" intake agent that Salesforce shipped with Agentforce Operations on April 29: an inbound submission triggers an agent that chases the submitter for missing fields until the file is complete enough to route. The same shape works for any small business that lives or dies on whether an inbound packet of information is complete the first time, which is most of insurance, healthcare billing, mortgage, and title.

1) The "in-good-order" intake agent that closes its own gaps. Salesforce broke out a bank loan example and an insurance claim example at the Agentforce Operations launch. Trigger is an inbound document or form. The agent reads the submission, checks against required fields, emails the submitter back in their voice asking for the eight missing items at once, parses replies, and only routes to a human when the file is complete. For a small commercial brokerage, that means the producer's submission email triggers an agent that reads the SOV and OCRs the loss runs, then sends one consolidated request for the missing items. Stack is shared inbox + extraction step + agency management system (AMS360, Applied Epic) + Claude for per-carrier rules. Replaces the CSR doing first-pass review. Same shape works for mortgage, title, and medical billing batch intake.

2) The dormant-customer reactivation cadence agent. Dental and vet practices are running this through Arini and DoctorConnect: nightly cron over the practice management database, pulls inactive patients, drafts a personalized SMS in the practice's voice with a deep-link booking calendar, parses replies. SMS-first because open rates run ~98% versus under 20% for email. Same shape lands in music studios on Opus1 (student hasn't booked in 21 days), gyms on Mindbody (member missing 30 days), independent counselors with a CRM full of past juniors, and medspas on Boulevard (filler interval lapsed). Replaces the part-time admin with a spreadsheet and a guilty conscience.

3) The denial-and-appeal agent for medical billing. Kyron Medical and Combine Health are doing this end-to-end. When an 835/277 denial hits the practice management system, the agent reads the denial code, pulls the supporting clinical documentation from the EHR, looks up the payer's specific medical-necessity language, drafts a payer-formatted appeal letter, and either submits via the clearinghouse or queues for biller signature. Also runs the loop for prior auths under the new CMS-0057-F 7-day rule. For a small physical therapy practice on WebPT or Prompt, that's same-day appeals in the payer's own language. Same shape works for solo psychiatrists, pediatric clinics, and DME suppliers.

4) The month-end close agent on a calendar trigger. OpenAI's accounting team uses one internally as part of Workspace Agents: three business days before close, the agent pulls the GL, runs reconciliations, flags variances, drafts explanations, and produces audit-ready workpapers. Translation to a 30-client bookkeeping firm: per-client calendar trigger, runs the QBO month-end checklist, generates the workpaper PDF, emails the controller the three things needing human eyes. 1.5 bookkeepers handle what used to take three. Same shape lands in small law firms (Clio trust reconciliation, billable-time-by-matter) and multi-provider medical practices.
